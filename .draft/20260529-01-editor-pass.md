The book is structurally coherent and the prose is strong. ## Tier 1 — Structural

### 1.1 The "walk each step in turn" promise is broken by the chapter order
`00_eleven_step_reference.md:19` — "The chapters that follow walk each one in turn — each chapter tagged, in italics under its title, with the step or steps it carries."

But the chapters are NOT in cycle order. Cycle order (from the reference page) is: Intent(1), Covenant(2), Stewardship(3), Specification(4), Line-upon-Line(5), Physical(6), Watching(7), Atonement(8), Sabbath(9), Consecration(10), Zion(11). The chapters carry the steps in this order:

| Chapter | Tag | Cycle step |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | *Specification* | 4 |
| 4 | *Watching* | 7 |
| 5 | (untagged) | — |
| 6 | *Covenant* | 2 |
| 7 | *Stewardship* | 3 |
| 8 | *Atonement* | 8 |
| 9 | *Context* | 5 |
| 10 | *Atonement* | 8 (again) |
| 11 | *Sabbath* | 9 |
| 12 | *Consecration · Zion* | 10, 11 |

A reader who takes "walk each one in turn" literally and tries to follow the cycle through the chapters hits Watching (7) before Covenant (2), then Atonement (8) before Context (5), then Atonement **again**. The order is thematically deliberate and good — but the reference-page language promises a linear walk the book doesn't deliver. **Move:** reword the reference page to say the chapters explore the steps thematically (not sequentially), and/or print the step *number* in each chapter tag so the non-linear mapping is visible rather than confusing. Cost: small.

### 1.2 Two chapters tagged *Atonement*, out of cycle order
Chapter 8 (*Atonement* — mechanics of refinement) and Chapter 10 (*Atonement* — softening) both carry the tag, with Chapter 9 (*Context*) between them. The two are genuinely different facets (active refinement vs. passive yielding), but the doubled tag with a different step between them is the sharpest instance of 1.1. **Move:** either differentiate the tags (e.g. *Atonement — Refinement* / *Atonement — Yielding*) or fold the explanation into the 1.1 reference-page rewording. Cost: small.

### 1.3 Glossary engineering-mappings contradict the rebuilt chapters
`15_glossary_of_fused_terms.md` was expanded this session but its original entries predate the 2026-05-29 chapter rebuilds and now fight them:
- **Zion** (line 14): "a peer-to-peer distributed consensus network or synchronized microservices architecture." But Chapter 12 (`12_conclusion_zion.md:33`) explicitly argues microservices are the **wrong** model — "That is the opposite of 'one heart and one mind.'" The glossary endorses exactly what the chapter refutes.
- **Heart-Softening** (line 13): "clearing execution buffers, invalidating stale cache connections, and releasing memory locks." But Chapter 10's rebuild **removed** the cache/connection-pool parallel and replaced it with gardening + the AI-mirror. The glossary still teaches the discarded parallel.
- **The Veil** (line 16): "API gateway or abstraction layer" — a colder, older mapping than Chapter 9's "anchor of hope within the veil."

**Move:** reconcile the glossary's engineering side against the rebuilt chapters — Zion → shared-intent ward-council multi-agent (not microservices); heart-softening → yielding assumed-clarity / the mirror (not cache locks). Cost: small-medium. This is the cleanest convergence candidate — I expect the AI-engineer reader to flag the Zion/microservices contradiction independently.

## Tier 2 — Voice & repetition (polish)

### 2.1 The imago-Dei close has become a verbatim refrain
The "shape rhymes / substances do not / same Author wrote both / only one of them is a soul" boundary appears as the closing move in Ch 5 (`:39`), Ch 8 (`:45`), Ch 10 (`:39`), and Ch 11 (`:41`). In 8, 10, and 11 the wording is nearly identical — "The shape rhymes. The substances do not. … The same Author wrote both." Read in sequence the three rebuilt chapters end on the same two sentences. The motif is doctrinally important (it's the Tier 1.4 imago-Dei boundary, applied per chapter on purpose) — but the CLAUDE.md cut-list bans closing refrains, and this now reads as one. **Move:** keep the boundary in all four, but vary the phrasing so it lands as a returning theme, not a copied stamp. Cost: small.

### 2.2 The Engineering-Parallel opening is templated across the rebuilds
Ch 8 (`:29`): "Scripture has carried the pattern of refinement-by-orientation for millennia. Engineering is reaching toward part of it now…" Ch 10 (`:27`): "Scripture has carried the asymmetry of softening for millennia." Ch 11 (`:31`): "Scripture has carried the structural cessation for millennia. Engineering is reaching toward part of it now…" Three Engineering Parallels open with the same sentence skeleton. **Move:** vary at least one or two openings. Cost: small. (Same root cause as 2.1 — the three were rebuilt in one sitting.)

### 2.3 "Older than the tool" / "small instance of an older law" is a verbal tic
Ch 0 (`:70` "The pattern is older than the tool"), Ch 3 (`:46` "The principle is older than the tool"), Ch 5 (`:39` "a small instance of the older law"), Ch 10 (`:39` "a small instance of an older mechanism"), Ch 12 (`:41`/`:43` "The pattern is older than the tool… The engineering catches up to the pattern. It does not invent it"). Good line; overused. **Move:** thin to two or three uses, the strongest placements. Cost: small.

### 2.4 Chapter 9 is ~2,950 words — nearly double the next-longest — and re-explains its own frame twice
`09_hope_and_the_veil.md` carries the three postures (Prescription/Rest/Proposal) AND the four groups AND the pride mechanism AND the Lamanite corrective AND hope geometry — then the Engineering Parallel re-walks the three postures and the four groups a second time in engineering register. It's excellent but it's a lot, and the double-walk (doctrine version, then engineering version, of both frames) is where the length comes from. **Move:** consider tightening the Engineering Parallel's re-statement of the three postures (the reader just read them), or splitting. Cost: medium. Flag, don't force — this may be the chapter readers love most.

### 2.5 The *Context* tag on Chapter 9 is a stretch
Chapter 9 is tagged *Context* (cycle step 5, Line-upon-Line), but the chapter is about hope, posture, the four groups, and pride — not about context-engineering / layered understanding. The tag will mislead a reader using tags to navigate the cycle. **Move:** reconsider the tag (it may belong to a different step, or want a more honest label). Tie to 1.1. Cost: small.
