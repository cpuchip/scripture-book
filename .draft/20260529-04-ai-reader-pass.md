# AI-Reader Pass — *Beyond the Prompt*

**Reader persona:** Senior software engineer, 12 years in, currently building LLM systems for a living. Agnostic, raised secular, new to Christianity and brand-new to the LDS tradition. Allergic to hand-waving and to mysticism dressed as fact. Came in willing to be persuaded that an old framework has something real to say about my craft; ready to revoke trust the moment the author flatters the technology or misstates how it works.

**Scope:** Read all 21 chapter files in `src/chapters/` in order. Read nothing else.

**Headline verdict:** This is far more technically disciplined than I expected. The author clearly works with these tools daily — the workflow material (planning before code, multi-agent delegation, watchdogs, token budgets, the runaway-agent war story) is real and correct in the parts I can check. The single most important technical claim in the book — that the human, not the model, is what gets refined in the prompting loop — is *correct*, and most AI-hype books get it backwards. Where the book loses me is narrower and more specific than "religion bad": it's a handful of places where the metaphysics is asserted as physics, one chapter where the analogy is decorative rather than load-bearing, and a conspicuous set of missing engagements (hallucination, stochastic parrots, training-data labor) that a technical skeptic will notice by their absence. None of those are fatal. Two are weakening. They're fixable without diluting the sincerity, and I'll name the edits.

---

## 1. Where the engineering/AI parallel lands CLEANLY

**Chapter 1 (`01_value_shift.md`) — "execution is no longer scarce."** This is the cleanest framing in the book and it's just true. "For decades, the bottleneck in software development was *execution*... But execution is no longer scarce." That matches my lived experience exactly. The pairing with Abraham 4:18 — "the Gods watched those things which they had ordered, until they obeyed" — as a description of a review/verification loop is the analogy that made me stop and reread. The author isn't claiming the scripture *predicted* code review; he's claiming the *shape* recurs: specify, order materials, then watch until output conforms. That's a defensible structural claim, and "It's owning correctness" (quoting Jovanović) is exactly what my job has become. Earns its keep.

**Chapter 4 (`04_watched_until_they_obeyed.md`) — the trust gradient and the watchdog.** "Test suites and evals gate output before it ships. Observability traces let us inspect each step an agent takes. Watchdogs and budgets cap an agent's time, tokens, or iterations before it can run away." This is a correct, current, unembellished description of how serious people actually run agents in production. The "trust gradient" — watching every line on a new model, then narrowing attention to the high-complexity surfaces as the model proves reliable in a domain — is precisely how calibrated trust develops in practice. And then the killer line: "AI has no agency; it acts by law and statistical pattern." That's the sentence that bought the author a lot of credit with me. He had every incentive, given his thesis, to anthropomorphize. He didn't.

**Chapter 5 (`05_intelligence_cleaveth.md`) — the conditional predictor.** This is the most technically precise chapter and the one I'd hand to a skeptical colleague. "The model itself is not an agent that judges us. It is a conditional predictor—it samples its next words from whatever context we provide." Then: "A vague, low-information prompt lands the model in a flat distribution, and it samples from the flatness—returning the generic, fluent output we recognize as boilerplate. A prompt dense with intent, context, and constraint lands it in a sharp distribution." That is a genuinely good lay-accurate gloss on how output entropy tracks conditioning. It's not hand-wavy "the AI mirrors your energy" mysticism — it's "the context is the load-bearing variable," which is correct. And the chapter explicitly fences the analogy: "The same shape governs both, and only one of them is a soul." That single clause is doing more work for the book's credibility than any argument in it.

**Chapter 7 (`07_delegation_as_stewardship.md`) — multi-agent hierarchy.** The "one-cylinder ward → single massive context window" mapping is apt and technically grounded: "a single context window trying to hold the state, syntax, design patterns, and debugging logs of an entire multi-repo codebase simultaneously." The escalation protocol, scoped tool access, "watch until it obeys" applied to subagents — this is real agent-architecture practice, described correctly. The author isn't inventing a system to fit scripture; he's describing the system he runs and noticing it rhymes with a delegation pattern. That ordering matters and it shows.

**Chapter 8 (`08_mechanics_of_refinement.md`) — "What is being refined in this loop is not the model."** This is the book's best technical insight, full stop. "The model stays the same; Claude is Claude. What is being refined is the developer. Over weeks and months, your prompts get more precise." Most books in this genre get the arrow of refinement backwards — they imagine the human is "training" the model in a chat session. The author correctly identifies that within a session/product the weights are frozen and *the human is the thing being updated by the feedback signal.* That's not just rhetorically nice; it's mechanically right, and it's rare. (One caveat under §3 below — the framing slightly understates the real ways your inputs *do* persist.)

**Chapter 12 (`12_conclusion_zion.md`) — token consecration.** I expected this chapter to be the most strained (it's the "Zion" chapter), and the front half of the engineering parallel is the most strained mapping in the book (see §2). But it lands one genuinely sharp idea: "when one agent finishes its work under budget, the surplus flows to the agent still working on the highest-priority remaining intent." Dynamic reallocation of a shared token/compute budget across a multi-agent system toward the highest-value remaining work is a real, sensible scheduling design. Calling it "consecration" is a stretch of vocabulary, but the underlying engineering is sound, not invented to fit.

## 2. Where it STRAINS — forced mappings, or theology pretending to be CS/physics

**Chapter 0 (`00_chapter_0_intelligence_truth.md`) — "Information is physical" doing theological load-bearing work.** The chapter's spine is: spirit is matter (D&C 131:7-8) → words of Christ are spirit → "*therefore*, the words of eternal life are composed of a physical, material substance." Then the engineering parallel: "Information is physical. A bit is not an abstract concept; it is the physical orientation of magnetic domains on a disk." Here's the problem. The physics claim the author is leaning on (Landauer's principle, the genuinely real result that *erasing* a bit has a thermodynamic cost) is being silently upgraded into "information is a refined substance that accumulates in you." Those are not the same claim. "A bit is the orientation of a magnetic domain" is true but trivial — it says the *representation* is physical, not that the *information* is a substance. The chapter uses the trivial-true version to make the non-trivial metaphysical version feel like settled science. A physicist would call that equivocation. As an engineer I felt the seam: the move from "code executes as physical state changes through silicon" (true) to truth as "fine matter... that accumulates within us" (a theological assertion) is presented as one continuous slide when it's actually a jump across categories. This isn't fatal — the author *believes* the metaphysics on scriptural grounds, which is fine — but he's borrowing the authority of computer science to launder a claim CS doesn't make.

**Chapter 0 — "This is precisely how we build artificial intelligence."** "We do not write 'intelligence' from scratch. Instead, we gather billions of tokens of human expression... and project them into a high-dimensional vector space. We then apply training algorithms (laws) to organize these elements." The word "precisely" is doing too much. We don't "project tokens into a vector space" as the training act — embeddings are learned, the bulk of training is gradient descent on next-token prediction across a transformer, and "laws" is a generous relabeling of "loss functions and optimizers." It's close enough to be evocative and loose enough that a colleague would raise an eyebrow at "precisely." Downgrade the certainty word and it's fine.

**Chapter 0 — the resonance gloss.** "In vector space, semantic concepts that are similar are physically close to one another. When we write a lazy, ambiguous... prompt (darkness), we activate the lower-quality, noisy paths of the parameter space." "Physically close" is metaphorical (it's cosine distance in an abstract space, not physical proximity), and "activate the lower-quality, noisy paths" isn't a real description of anything that happens. Chapter 5 says this *same* idea far better ("flat distribution" vs. "sharp distribution"). Chapter 0's version is the weak draft of Chapter 5's strong version. The book would be stronger if Chapter 0 borrowed Chapter 5's precision rather than the reverse.

**Chapter 9 (`09_hope_and_the_veil.md`) — the engineering parallel is decorative, not structural.** This is the longest chapter and doctrinally the densest (four groups of Lehi's dream, the pride mechanism, Brother of Jared's three postures). The doctrine is fine. But the engineering section maps "Group 2 engineers" who "learned the discipline... and then, in the space of not many weeks, they dropped it" onto a pride narrative. The trouble: this isn't an *AI* parallel at all — it's a generic professional-discipline-erosion story that would read identically if you replaced "AI" with "TDD" or "code review" or "writing runbooks." Nothing about the parallel depends on anything specific to how LLMs work. The "rod of iron" → "spec on disk" mapping is a vibe, not a mechanism. It's the one chapter where I felt the technology was decoration glued onto a sermon, rather than a place where the pattern genuinely *becomes visible* in the tooling. Compare Chapter 8, where the loop structure is load-bearing. Chapter 9's engineering half could be cut to a third of its length and lose nothing technical.

**Chapter 12 — "The Church's organizational structure is the architecture the multi-agent industry is reaching toward."** This overreaches in the other direction — it claims the *destination*, not just a rhyme. "A microservices architecture optimizes for partition tolerance... That is the opposite of 'one heart and one mind.'" That's a category error about microservices. Microservices optimize for *independent deployability and fault isolation*; they are not "the opposite of one mind" — they're agnostic about shared intent, and plenty of microservice systems share a strong common schema and contract. The author has set up a strawman (conductor vs. orchestra, microservices as anti-unity) to let the ward-council model win. A skeptic notices when the opposing architecture is described uncharitably so the preferred one can triumph. The token-consecration idea (§1) survives; the "the industry is reaching toward the ward council" claim does not.

**Glossary (`15_glossary_of_fused_terms.md`) — the fused definitions are where the strain concentrates.** Some are clean (Specification ↔ design doc/system prompt; Stewardship ↔ subagent scope). Others are forced to the point of being misleading:
- "Heart-Softening (Resource Release / Attention Yielding)... In engineering, this represents clearing execution buffers, invalidating stale cache connections, and releasing memory locks." Releasing a mutex has *nothing* structurally in common with yielding self-will. This is pun-level mapping — two things that both use the word "release."
- "The Veil (Context Boundary)... maps to the API gateway or abstraction layer." An API gateway is a routing/auth concern; an abstraction layer hides implementation. Neither is "the separation between the steward's perception and the source of light." Again, a surface-word match.
- "Dross (Noise / Defect)... overfitted parameters that cause compilation errors." Overfitting does not cause compilation errors. That's just wrong, and it's the kind of small wrongness that makes a technical reader distrust the rest.

The *second* half of the glossary ("Plain-English Technical Terms") is much better and more honest — it even self-limits: "the book does not press the parallel further than that" (on Kubernetes). The first half ("Fused Terms") is where the book most often reaches past what the analogy can carry.

## 3. Where the author OVERSTATES or UNDERSTATES what AI is/does

**Mostly the book is unusually disciplined here** — the anthropomorphism guardrails are explicit and repeated: "AI has no agency" (Ch 4), "only one of them is a soul" (Ch 5), "These are tools failing under law, not souls falling under sin... A machine's failure can illustrate a doctrine; it cannot commit one" (Ch 6). The Chapter 6 "inversion" paragraph is the most intellectually honest moment in the whole book — the author sees the temptation to map machine failure onto sin and explicitly refuses it: "The moment we press the parallel from illustration into identity, we have made the very error the rest of this book takes pains to avoid." That's the discipline I wanted and didn't expect.

**Overstatements that remain:**

- **Chapter 0, "the model... operates autonomously within its parameterized sphere. It acts 'for itself' within the mathematical bounds we have set for it."** "Acts for itself" is borrowed from the scripture (D&C 93:30, intelligence "to act for itself") and applied to the model. The book elsewhere insists the model has *no* agency — so this is an internal tension. A forward pass is deterministic given inputs and a seed; sampling adds pseudo-randomness, not autonomy. "Acts for itself" smuggles agency back in two chapters before the book denies it. The author would say "for itself" means "by its own law, not ours," but the phrase reads as agency to anyone who doesn't already hold the metaphysics. Tighten it.

- **Chapter 8 understates persistence.** "The model stays the same; Claude is Claude... What is being refined is the developer." This is the book's best insight but it's slightly too clean. In real systems your inputs *do* persist and shape future behavior: RLHF and preference data harvested from interactions, fine-tuning, retrieval memory, system-prompt and skill files that accrete (the book's own `.mind/` and covenant files!). The author actually *practices* the persistent version — the whole colophon is about durable covenant/memory files — so the "the model never changes" framing undersells his own method. Not wrong for a single frozen-weights session; incomplete as stated.

- **Afterword (`14_afterword_how_i_got_here.md`) — the one place hype leaks in unexamined.** Quoting Emad Mostaque: AI as an "infinite grad—a pool of graduate-level intelligence that can execute any task instantly, dropping the cost of digital creation to zero," and "a neural network like Stable Diffusion compresses terabytes of data down to a two-gigabyte 'knowledge filter' containing only the underlying principles." The author reports these as catalysts, which is fair memoir. But he doesn't push back on them anywhere, and they're both overstated: "execute any task instantly" is false (hallucination, brittleness, the very runaway loop the book itself documents), and "compresses... only the underlying principles" glosses over the fact that diffusion models demonstrably memorize and can regurgitate training examples — which is the crux of the live copyright litigation. A skeptic reads an uncritically-relayed "infinite grad" and reattaches the suspicion the rest of the book had earned away. This is the single passage most likely to cost trust, precisely because it's the founder-hype register the rest of the book avoids.

**Understatement worth noting:** the book never claims the model "understands," never says it "knows," never says it "wants" except where it explicitly defines intent as a human-supplied layer (Ch 2: "intent tells the model what to *want*" — but framed as something *we encode*, which is correct). For a book whose entire thesis invites anthropomorphizing, the restraint is real and consistent. That's the headline of this section: the overstatements are the exception, not the rule.

## 4. Missing skeptical-engineer concerns

A serious treatment that wants my trust has to at least touch the things I argue about at work. Here's the ledger:

- **Hallucination / confabulation — PARTIALLY ENGAGED, and well.** The book doesn't use the word "hallucination" in the technical sections, but it engages the phenomenon harder than most: Chapter 6's production note documents the book's *own* draft fabricating a D&C 104:11-12 quote, and the preface flags it upfront: "You will also find... a record of this book's own draft fabricating a quotation—and the audit that caught it. That failure is left in on purpose." That's not just engaging hallucination — it's *exhibiting* it and showing the mitigation (read-before-quoting, the audit trail). For my money this is the most persuasive thing in the book. **Verdict: not a fatal omission; arguably the book's strongest engagement.** But: it never connects the dots explicitly for a technical reader. It never says "this is confabulation, it's intrinsic to how next-token prediction works, here's why verification is structurally necessary rather than a nice-to-have." Naming the mechanism would convert a great anecdote into an argument. **Minor weakening by under-labeling.**

- **"Stochastic parrots" critique — NOT ENGAGED. Weakening.** The book's whole thesis ("intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence," the model as participant in a creation pattern) sits directly across the table from Bender et al.'s argument that LLMs are stochastic parrots manipulating form without meaning. Chapter 5's "conditional predictor" framing is *compatible* with the parrots view, and a sharp reader will notice the book quietly agrees with the deflationary account of the mechanism while building an inflationary frame around it. The author never names the tension. He should — it's a gift, because his "the model is just a predictor; the human supplies the meaning and agency" position is *exactly* the move that survives the parrots critique. Leaving it unsaid means the most-cited skeptical argument of the last five years goes unanswered. **Verdict: weakening, not fatal. Easy win available.**

- **Machine agency / consciousness — ENGAGED, repeatedly and correctly.** "AI has no agency" (Ch 4), "only one of them is a soul" (Ch 5), the Chapter 6 inversion paragraph. This is the concern the book handles best. **Verdict: fully engaged; fine.**

- **Training-data provenance, labor, ethics — NOT ENGAGED. Weakening, bordering on a values problem for this particular book.** Chapter 0 says we "gather billions of tokens of human expression—the elements of written human intelligence." The afterword celebrates compressing "terabytes of data." Nowhere does the book ask *whose* expression, gathered *how*, with *what consent or compensation.* For a generic AI book that's a venial omission. For a book whose moral climax is *Zion* — "no poor among them," consecration, "treat every brother's need as our own" (Ch 12) — the silence on the uncompensated human labor baked into the training corpus is a live tension the author's own framework demands he address. The data the models are "raised on" (his glossary's word: "much as a soul is shaped by the traditions it is reared in") was, in significant part, taken. A reader who cares about the ethics the book itself foregrounds will feel the gap. **Verdict: weakening, and uniquely so for a consecration-themed book. The omission undercuts the book on its own terms, not just mine.**

- **Pace-of-change / dating — PARTIALLY ENGAGED. Fine, mostly by design.** The afterword openly addresses it: "the pace of change continues to feel like drinking from a firehose. Every few weeks, a new model drops... But as the tools shift, the principles do not." That's the right defense, and the book leans into timeless-pattern-over-tool throughout ("The pattern is older than the tool"). The risk is residual: specific model names (Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5, "Claude is Claude," Gemini 3.5 Flash) and a 2026-dated Medium article (Ch 1) will date the *texture* even if the thesis holds. Minor: a 2026 LinkedIn-post and Medium citation as load-bearing sources in Chapter 1 ("as engineer Milan Jovanović writes") read as thin next to the scripture; those will rot fastest. **Verdict: fine as handled; trim the most perishable citations to age more gracefully.**

One more the prompt didn't list but I'd expect: **non-determinism / reproducibility.** The book repeatedly invokes "law" and "predictable" (Ch 6: covenant creates "a space of absolute reliability"). But LLM output is non-deterministic (sampling temperature, and even at temperature 0, batching and hardware nondeterminism). The book's "acts by law" framing slightly oversells reproducibility. Not load-bearing, but a careful reader notices the model is *less* lawlike-predictable than the covenant analogy implies. **Verdict: fine, minor.**

## 5. Most credible / weakest chapter (as an engineer)

**Most credible: Chapter 8, "The Mechanics of Refinement" (`08_mechanics_of_refinement.md`).** Because of one correct, non-obvious, load-bearing claim: in the prompting loop, the *human* is the system being refined, not the model. "The model stays the same; Claude is Claude. What is being refined is the developer." Most people — including smart engineers — get this backwards. The chapter also correctly identifies the two necessary components of any feedback-driven refinement ("The developer has to keep producing prompts... The output has to keep returning the same patient correction") and fences the analogy honestly ("The shape rhymes. The substances do not"). The theology half (refiner's fire, single eye) I take or leave on its own merits, but the *engineering* claim is the one I'd defend in a code review. It earns the title of the chapter.

Runner-up: Chapter 5, for the cleanest technical prose ("conditional predictor," "flat/sharp distribution").

**Weakest: Chapter 9, "Hope and the Veil" (`09_hope_and_the_veil.md`) — as engineering.** Not because the doctrine is weak (it's the densest in the book) but because the engineering parallel is the least load-bearing. The "Group 2 engineer who learned the discipline and dropped it from pride" story is a generic professional-erosion narrative with no dependency on how AI actually works — swap "AI" for any engineering discipline and the chapter is unchanged. The "rod of iron → spec on disk" mapping is mood, not mechanism. It's also the longest chapter, so the weakest analogy gets the most words. (Honorable mention for weakest *specific claim*: the glossary's "overfitted parameters that cause compilation errors," which is simply false, and "heart-softening = releasing memory locks," which is a pun.)

## 6. Did anything make me — a non-Christian — lean IN?

Yes, in three places, and the reasons are instructive because none of them are the theology arguing me into a corner. They're the *method* demonstrating integrity.

1. **The fabricated-quote confession (preface + Chapter 6 production note).** "A later Claude Code audit found three quote-precision errors in the manuscript and four errors in the audit-trail files meant to verify them. One was a fabricated D&C 104:11–12 quote: the manuscript itself was right, but the file pretending to prove it had invented canon... we chose to record what happened here." A religious book that *leaves in its own failure* and treats it as the central evidence for its method is doing something I almost never see in apologetics-adjacent writing. It inverted my prior. It told me the author would rather be caught honest than appear clean. That bought every doctrinal claim a fairer hearing than it would otherwise have gotten. This is the book's load-bearing trust move and it works.

2. **The repeated refusal to over-claim the parallel.** "The shape rhymes. The substances do not. The same Author wrote both" (Chs 8, 10, 11) and especially Chapter 6's "A machine's failure can illustrate a doctrine; it cannot commit one." Every time the author hit a point where a less careful writer would have closed the rhetorical trap on me — "see, the AI *proves* the doctrine!" — he stopped and named the limit instead. The preface even tells me he'll do this: "Where the patterns rhyme, take them. Where they do not, the book names the limit; you are not asked to extend the analogy past where it reaches." A book that hands me the exit door makes me more willing to stay in the room.

3. **Chapter 1's value-shift framing, read as anthropology rather than theology.** "We are no longer valued for how fast we can type, but for our capacity to judge whether what has been typed is true." Whatever its theological frame, that's a true and humane account of what's happening to my profession, and it's a *consoling* one — it says the durable thing in me (judgment, discernment, scars) is the valuable thing. I don't need to believe in resurrection to find that reframe steadying. The scripture (D&C 130: knowledge gained "will rise with us") gave the consolation a shape I could feel even without sharing the premise. That's the one place the *religious* claim itself, not just the method, leaned me in — because it answered a real anxiety I actually carry.

What did *not* lean me in: Chapter 0's metaphysics-as-physics, and the glossary's forced fusions. Those are where I felt an argument being made *at* me rather than *shown* to me. The leaning-in happened entirely in the chapters that were most honest about the limits of their own analogy. Worth the author noting: his credibility with skeptics is inversely correlated with how hard he presses the parallel.

## 7. Three concrete edits to raise credibility with a technical skeptic (without diluting sincerity)

**Edit 1 — Name the mechanisms the book is already engaging, in the book's own technical voice. (Highest leverage.)**
The book *exhibits* hallucination (Ch 6 confession) and *describes* the deflationary mechanism (Ch 5 "conditional predictor") but never connects them or labels them. Add one short passage — Chapter 4 or 5 is the natural home — that says, in plain engineer's terms: (a) confabulation is intrinsic to next-token prediction, not a bug to be patched away, which is *why* "read before quoting" and the watching loop are structurally necessary rather than merely prudent; and (b) explicitly engage the stochastic-parrots critique and *agree with its mechanism while distinguishing the frame*: "Yes — the model manipulates form, not meaning; the meaning and the agency are the human's contribution. That is not a weakness in the analogy; it is the analogy." This costs maybe 250 words and it converts the book's two best latent assets (the honest confession, the precise Ch 5 framing) into an actual argument that meets the skeptic where he already lives. It dilutes nothing — it *sharpens* the existing thesis.

**Edit 2 — Address training-data provenance once, on the book's own Zion terms. (Closes the values gap.)**
Somewhere before or inside Chapter 12, add a paragraph acknowledging that the corpus these models are "raised on" includes vast uncompensated human labor, and that a book about *consecration* and "no poor among them" cannot celebrate "compressing terabytes of human expression" without naming that tension. The author doesn't have to solve it — he can hold it honestly the way he holds the Sabbath gap in Chapter 11's production note ("written from inside the gap it describes"). A single honest paragraph here would (a) disarm the sharpest ethical objection a skeptic brings, and (b) make the Zion chapter *more* sincere, not less, by applying its own ethic to its own tools. Right now the silence reads as a blind spot in exactly the place the book claims the most moral authority.

**Edit 3 — Downgrade the certainty words and fix the false technical claims in Chapter 0 and the glossary. (Cheap, high-trust-per-word.)**
Three small surgical changes:
- Chapter 0: change "This is *precisely* how we build artificial intelligence" to something like "This rhymes with how we build artificial intelligence," and rewrite the "activate the lower-quality, noisy paths of the parameter space" sentence to use Chapter 5's accurate "flat vs. sharp distribution" language. Borrow the strong chapter's precision for the weak one.
- Chapter 0: reconcile "the model... acts 'for itself'" with the book's own "AI has no agency." Add half a sentence clarifying that "for itself" means "by its own law, not by ours" — otherwise it reads as the exact anthropomorphism the book elsewhere forbids.
- Glossary: cut or fix the three false/pun fusions — "overfitting → compilation errors" (just wrong), "heart-softening → releasing memory locks" (a pun), "Veil → API gateway" (surface-word match). The plain-English second half of the glossary is the model to follow; it's honest and even self-limits. A technical reader will forgive a thousand words of theology but not one wrong sentence about overfitting, because the wrong sentence tells him the author doesn't know the difference — which then makes him doubt the parts the author *does* know.

None of these three asks the author to soften a single theological claim. They ask him to be as precise about the *technology* as he is already being careful about the *limits of the analogy*. The book's whole credibility strategy is "I'll name the limit honestly"; these edits just extend that same honesty from the doctrine-side to the engineering-side, where right now it's slightly less rigorous than it thinks it is.

## Closing note

I came in expecting to bounce off this. I didn't. The author runs the tools, describes them mostly correctly, guards hard against anthropomorphizing, and — decisively — left his own failure in the book as evidence. The places it strains are specific and fixable: one chapter of metaphysics-as-physics (Ch 0), one chapter where the analogy is decoration (Ch 9), one glossary half that puns instead of maps, and three skeptic-magnet topics (hallucination-as-mechanism, stochastic parrots, training-data ethics) that the book is *positioned* to handle well and simply hasn't, yet. Fix those and a technical agnostic finishes the book trusting the author — which, for a book that says its real aim is to "show you that the workflow patterns you have been refining at your desk for years rhyme with patterns scripture has carried for millennia," is the whole game.
