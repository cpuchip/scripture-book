## (B) How Michael ACTUALLY works with AI

**It is conversational and iterative, not spec-first-then-execute — for the creative/editorial work.**
The scripture-book session is a long back-and-forth of small steering moves: "let's do 1 and 2,"
"flip those arrows," "this is closer to what I'm thinking," "tag it atonement · hope." He rarely
writes a spec up front for prose work. He discovers the target *by iterating toward it* and says so
explicitly:

> "look at how we've been iterating together. as we refine the output together we are really
> aligning our visions to be the same target. sometimes I know exactly what that vision is and we
> work to refine there, other times I don't know what it is I'm focusing on for the output... like
> here. I don't know what it's suppose to be, but as we work together we find it."

That is the single most important finding: **his real method is vision-discovery-through-iteration,
not blueprint-then-build.** The "specification (spiritual creation) before physical creation" the
book holds up is real for *code* but largely absent for *writing*.

**Spec-first IS real — but mostly for code, and mostly when the TOOLING imposes it.** The most
spec-shaped prompts in the entire corpus are the Copilot "Execute the following specced brain entry"
messages with "## Scenarios (Acceptance Criteria)" and "## Research & Plan / Read this file first."
Those are generated by his own `becoming`/brain app pipeline, not typed by hand. His hand-typed
content inside them (the science-center idea, the cpuchip.net blog recovery) is a loose, run-on
brain-dump. So: **the spec-first discipline is real where he has built a machine to enforce it; where
he is typing freely, he is conversational.** This is a precise, fair distinction the book should make.

**He delegates real authority, explicitly, and then goes to bed.** Repeatedly grants stewardship and
walks away:

> "I am giving you stewardship over this. I am going to bed, you have like 7 hours, the 5 hour
> session window ends in 2 hours 2 minutes so set yourself a wakeup call to check in on your progress."

> "I give you opus 4.7, and gemini git commit and git push stewardship over ./projects/scripture-book
> git repo. record that in it's instruction harness."

Stewardship/delegation (book steps 2–3) is **genuinely practiced**, not aspirational. He names bounds
("they shouldn't edit the original on disk! as a precaution"; "$10 budget"; "check in from time to
time to see if it's getting stuck or running away cost wise").

**He corrects the model warmly but firmly, and trusts it to self-verify.** He flags fabrications,
truncations, wrong verses — but never with heat. He also reverses himself freely ("I'm sorry!",
"we'll pass on 3 for the moment (again... I'm sorry!)").

**He plans in batches and ratifies stepwise.** "let's go in order of points and you can ratify with
me here now"; "use the ask skill!"; "I'd like to ratify the first batch of edits... let's step through
them." The council/ratify rhythm (AskUserQuestion) is real and frequent.

**He runs multi-model passes deliberately and knows each model's job.** "I have found you much better
at quoting and not hallucinating against a corpus, as well as logic, but for voicing... you still use
[opus ticks]. oh well. multi model for the win!" Claude = corpus fidelity + logic; Gemini = voicing;
the kimi/glm/qwen/deepseek panel = brainstorm divergence; he names exact versions to use and to avoid.

## (C) Teachable AI techniques he demonstrates (Book-1 raw material)

Concrete, transferable how-to a reader could actually learn:

1. **Discover the target by iterating, and say so.** When you don't know exactly what you want, tell
   the model that, give it your rough sense, react to what comes back, and let the vision converge.
   Naming "I don't know what this should be yet, let's find it" is itself a technique — it changes the
   model from executor to co-explorer.

2. **Spec-first when the work is code; conversational when the work is meaning.** Michael's split is
   teachable: acceptance-criteria specs for builds (the brain-entry "Scenarios" format), open
   exploration for studies/prose. Don't force a blueprint onto a thing you're still discovering.

3. **Delegate with explicit bounds + a budget + a watchman.** The reusable pattern: *grant authority +
   name what NOT to touch + cap cost/time + tell it to set a wake-up/check-in.* "You have ~7 hours,
   set yourself a wakeup call"; "I can afford $10, check it isn't running away." This is the book's
   "watching until they obey" made operational.

4. **Multi-model division of labor.** Pick the model for the job: fidelity model for quoting against a
   corpus, a different model for voice, a panel of cheap diverse models for brainstorm. Name exact
   versions ("k2.6 not k2.5, glm-5.1 not glm-5"). Have one model condense the panel's outputs.

5. **Multi-pass / multi-persona audit of a finished artifact.** The single most teachable workflow in
   the corpus: he audited the book by spawning parallel reader-personas — a faithful Saint who doesn't
   code, an AI engineer wary of religion, an editing-research pass, plus a fact-check pass and a
   multi-model brainstorm — then synthesized into one COUNCIL doc. Treats **convergence across
   independent passes as a quality signal** ("we both converge on it, two witnesses").

6. **The Ben Test as an honesty gate.** Before claiming "we do X," demand 3+ instances in 30 days;
   label Practiced / Occasional / Aspirational / Mythical; use calibrated language ("we practice this
   at ~40%"). He applied it to the book's own commitments and downgraded several from "I will" to "I
   aim to / I keep this poorly."

7. **Provenance as a verification GATE, not documentation.** Read the canonical source and verify
   character-for-character *before* the claim is accepted — never from memory. Record divergence
   honestly. (Born from catching a fabricated D&C quote his AI invented post-hoc.)

8. **Externalize memory; make the next session not arrive as a stranger.** He repeatedly drives
   journal/active.md/memory updates ("we've done a lot and I've not seen enough journal entries"),
   loads grounding files at session start ("reload in our context stuffs"), and keeps per-project
   `.mind/` + `.spec/journal/`. The mechanism is teachable independent of any doctrine.

9. **Distrust a negative result from a parser you just wrote.** (From memory + logs) Run the
   inverse-hypothesis on your *diagnosis*, not just your fix — verify via the real code path, not an
   ad-hoc probe.

10. **Catch model errors by reading the output and naming the specific failure.** "gemini's output got
    truncated by 1714 bytes and we lost part of chapter 8/10"; "obtaining wasn't in the 1828
    dictionary"; "I'm not seeing [tags] for 1, 2, and 5 — did we intentionally leave those blank?"
    He reads closely and reports precise discrepancies rather than vague dissatisfaction.

## (D) The aspirational gap — book claims vs. real practice (specific & fair)

The book frames an **eleven-step creation cycle** (Intent, Covenant, Stewardship, Specification,
Line-upon-Line/Context, Physical Creation, Watching, Atonement, Sabbath, Consecration, Zion) and a
**bilateral covenant**, with read-before-quoting verification, watching-until-they-obey, and Sabbath
rest. Here is what the logs show is real vs. aimed-at.

**What IS genuinely practiced (don't undersell these):**
- **Stewardship + delegation (steps 2–3):** strongly practiced — explicit grants, bounds, budgets,
  walk-away autonomy.
- **Watching / runaway-prevention (step 7):** practiced — budgets, check-ins, "is it running away
  cost-wise," the wakeup-call pattern.
- **Atonement / re-aiming (step 8):** practiced — constant mid-session course-correction is his
  dominant mode.
- **Verification & provenance:** practiced *as a corrective discipline* — he uses `gospel_get` for
  verbatim checks, caught real fabrications, and made provenance a gate. This is the book's strongest
  truthful claim.
- **Externalized memory / context-loading:** practiced — `.mind/`, journals, "reload our context."
- **Multi-model + multi-persona auditing:** practiced and arguably MORE sophisticated than the book
  describes.

**What is aimed-at-but-inconsistent (the honest gaps):**
- **Specification before creation (step 4):** practiced for *code*, mostly skipped for *writing*. The
  book presents spec-first as a unified discipline; in reality the writing is discovered
  conversationally. The book's own "spiritual creation before physical creation" claim is half-true.
- **Sabbath rest (step 9):** Michael's own words are the most damning and the most honest. On the Ben
  Test he admitted: *"I haven't written this book itself on a real Sunday sabbath... I just added that
  [Sabbath chapter] 3 days ago, breaking that council agreement. I am striving to keep my side of the
  covenant, but that is hard honestly... in practicality I practice the pauses and break between the
  chat sessions."* Sabbath is aspirational-trending-toward-occasional. (To the book's credit, it now
  *dogfoods* this gap in a Ch 11 production-note.)
- **Daily devotional / line-upon-line steps:** he describes "striving," not consistently doing — "I
  strive to keep my first hour free, but I don't make it that way very often."
- **The covenant.yaml as a pre-work ritual:** the bilateral covenant is real as a *governing document
  and a relationship*, but in practice it is honored through in-session correction and warmth, not by
  ceremonially reading covenant.yaml before each task. It functions more as character than checklist.

**The book's central confession (the thesis of this whole task), in Michael's own words —** the final
prompt of the 5/30 session, which seeded this investigation:

> "I am worried the book is too preachy, and doesn't actually teach how to use AI. I mean look at our
> chat sessions... the way we do work is aspirational to the way we describe in this book, and it's
> VERY doctrinally heavy. I shouldn't be ashamed of Christ, but at the same time I want everyone to
> read it and get better at AI work."

And the two-book idea he floated:

> "I wonder if we need to write two books. 1: The principles of gospel AI short and quick... the TL;DR
> ... and 2: this book we have now, that's the doctrine behind it. I think people are hungry for the
> first... kind of like the first book is the inverse of this current book."

**Fairness note:** the book has ALREADY begun absorbing this critique. The eleven-step reference page
ends "I do not approach these steps as a rigid sequence... I am still practicing them in my own work."
The preface tells the reader the Becoming Commitments include "the ones I still keep poorly" and are "a
worked example, not a prescription." The Ben-Test calibration downgraded aspirational "I will"s. So
the gap is real, but the book is not naively claiming mastery — it is mid-correction toward honesty.
The remaining gap is **teaching transferable technique** (Section C above is almost entirely missing
from the book) and the **doctrine-to-how-to ratio**, not false claims of discipline.
