# Practice 2 · Council Before You Build

Early on, I asked the AI to write a section of a study we'd been building for weeks. It wrote it fast and wrote it well — clean prose, confident, plausible. The only problem was that it contradicted something we had settled three documents earlier. I caught it, but only because I had read the earlier work and the contradiction snagged on something I remembered. If I'd been moving faster, it would have sailed straight into the manuscript wearing the same confidence as everything true around it.

That section was wrong for a specific reason: it had been *built* without being *counseled*. The model hadn't checked what we already knew, and I hadn't asked it to. We had skipped the step where you stop, look at what exists, lay out the options, and agree on the shape before a word gets written. That near-miss is the reason we wrote a covenant, a short file that says, among other things: *check existing work before you claim, and surface tensions instead of building only toward the answer.*

The second practice is: **council before you build.** Before the work starts, take counsel. Have the model lay out two or three ways to go and the trade-offs between them. Make it check what already exists. Then ratify one path, out loud and on purpose, before a line is written. The plan you reach is not bureaucracy. It is the spiritual creation: you make the thing in counsel before you make it in fact, and the making-in-counsel is what keeps the making-in-fact from collapsing.

But a plan has a shadow, and I learned it the hard way too. One of the first real projects I tried to build with AI was a small tool to search video transcripts and link back to the source. I sat down and produced a beautiful eight-milestone roadmap, then stared at it, buried. I wrote in the log that I felt overwhelmed by how much work was left. The plan was thorough enough to become a wall.

Both failures are the same lesson seen from two sides. Skip the council and you build on contradictions. Worship the plan and you never build at all. **A plan is a map, not a debt.** Council until you can see the way; then let the map serve the walking. You are allowed to revise it the moment the ground disagrees.

Today the practice runs like this, and the mechanics will age. An idea arrives, we talk it into options, the model checks the existing work and the open questions, and we ratify a short sequence of phases as questions: *do this first, then this, does that handle the edge case?* Then I set it loose. The questions run both ways. I ask the model what already exists; the model asks me to choose between the options it surfaced. The agreement we reach is the plan.

The shape under it is older than any of this. Before the world was made, "the Gods took counsel among themselves" ([Abraham 4:26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md)) [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md) and only then went down to form it; the creation existed spiritually before it existed naturally ([Moses 3:5](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/moses/3.md)). Counsel precedes creation. It always has. (Part Two: Council & Covenant · Spiritual Creation — the doctrine under this practice.)

**Try this.** Before your next real build, refuse to start cold. Ask the model: *what are two or three ways to do this, what does each cost, and what in our existing work bears on it?* Pick one, and say why. Then hold the plan loosely. It's a map you drew to find the way, not a contract you owe.

> **Remember:** Council before you build — surface the options, check what exists, ratify the path before a word is written. The plan is the spiritual creation, made in counsel before it's made in fact. But a plan is a map, not a debt: build *from* it, revise *with* it, and don't let its size become the reason you never start.
