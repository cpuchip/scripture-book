# Chapter 7: Delegation as Stewardship

**Binding Question:** How do we delegate authority and execute work without falling into the trap of offloading responsibility?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And let every man be accountable of his stewardship, unto me and to the church in all things. ... And a commandment I give unto you, that ye shall organize yourselves and appoint every man his stewardship;..."
> — [Doctrine and Covenants 104:11–12](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/104.md)

---

## The Core Reframe

[Outline: Delegation is a divine principle. God delegates stewardship of the earth to mankind. But stewardship is not offloading. To delegate as stewardship means assigning a defined sphere of responsibility, establishing clear boundaries, and requiring regular accountability reports. In the Jethro pattern (Exodus 18), Moses delegated the small matters but remained accountable for the large. A steward who is left without oversight will drift; a delegator who micromanages destroys agency. The balance is structured accountability.]

---

## The Engineering Parallel

[Outline: We apply this when structuring AI agents. Rather than asking a single agent to "build my app" (which fails due to scope drift), we define specific agent stewardships. We assign one agent to research, one to plan, and one to verify. We enforce boundaries (scopes) and require the agent to report its decisions at designated gates. The human retains the vision and final judgment, while the agent owns the execution within its steward-sphere.]

---

## Becoming Commitment

[Outline: I commit to defining clear spheres of responsibility in my calling and family duties, delegating with trust but maintaining regular, structured check-ins. I will not offload my responsibility to verify outcomes.]
