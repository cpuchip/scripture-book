# Chapter 9: Hope and the Veil

*Atonement · Hope*

**Binding Question:** How do the three stewardship patterns of the Brother of Jared (Prescription, Rest, and Proposal) and the geometry of Hope guide a steward's active agency?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And behold, we have seen in this record that one of these was the brother of Jared; for so great was his faith in God, that when God put forth his finger he could not hide it from the sight of the brother of Jared, because of his word which he had spoken unto him, which word he had obtained by faith."
> — [Ether 12:20](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.md) [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.md?verse=20)

## The Core Reframe

The previous chapter named the mechanism of refinement: orientation plus patient correction, working in a loop, against a standard the soul did not write. What it did not unpack is the soul's part of that loop. Keeping the eye fixed on the prototype is not single. It has three faces — three distinct postures the steward must hold simultaneously — and the Brother of Jared embodies all three.

His three problems map to three distinct stewardship structures: Prescription, Rest, and Proposal.

The first problem was air. Without it, the occupants of the barges would suffocate in the closed vessels. The Lord's solution was a strict, non-negotiable prescription: "Behold, thou shalt make a hole in the top, and also in the bottom; and when thou shalt suffer for air thou shalt unstop the hole and receive air. And if it be so that the water come in upon thee, behold, ye shall stop the hole, that ye may not perish in the flood" ([Ether 2:20](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/2.md)). The prescription represents the outer boundaries of celestial law — the daily discipline of the commandments. *Drill here, open now, close then.* It is not open to steward improvisation. When a steward follows the prescription exactly, the structural integrity of the vessel is preserved.

The second problem was steering. The barges had no helm, rudder, or sails, yet the Brother of Jared did not ask about steering in his second prayer. The Lord addressed it anyway: "For behold, ye shall be as a whale in the midst of the sea; for the mountain waves shall dash upon you. Nevertheless, I will bring you up again out of the depths of the sea; for the winds have gone forth out of my mouth, and also the rains and the floods have I sent forth" ([Ether 2:24](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/2.md)). The wind that drove the barges across the ocean was already going forth before the steward boarded. The atoning grace of Christ is the same — the passive, constant pull that quickens the universe and draws the soul home. The steward cannot build a rudder to navigate the infinite ocean of eternity. The steward's part is to enter the vessel, rest in this established grace, and sing praises.

The third problem was light. The Lord refused to prescribe an answer. He asked: "What will ye that I should do that ye may have light in your vessels?" ([Ether 2:23](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/2.md)). He constrained the question by ruling out windows and fire, then threw it back to the steward. The Brother of Jared responded by climbing a high mountain, melting sixteen small stones out of a rock, and presenting them: "touch these stones, O Lord, with thy finger, and prepare them that they may shine forth in darkness; and they shall shine forth unto us in the vessels which we have prepared, that we may have light while we shall cross the sea" ([Ether 3:4](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/3.md)). Opaque rock cannot transmit light. To receive the light of truth, the steward must labor, molting the stones until they are "white and clear, even as transparent glass" ([Ether 3:1](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/3.md)). The proposal pattern is how active agency brings its refined substance to the Master, allowing Him to touch and quicken what we have prepared.

These three postures are not three separate phases of stewardship; they are three faces of one stewardship under sanctification. The Brother of Jared held all three simultaneously. He honored the prescriptions, he rested in the grace already in motion, and he proposed his stones. Drop any one and the stewardship fails. A steward who keeps the prescription but refuses the rest is striving against the wind. A steward who rests in grace but skips the proposal never brings their own labor to the Master. A steward who proposes but ignores the prescription is improvising the foundation.

But the question this chapter has not yet asked is: *who, exactly, holds these three postures, and what happens to those who do not?* Lehi's vision answered this question centuries before Moroni's record reached us. The vision describes four groups, four distinct ways a soul can relate to the standard ([1 Nephi 8:21-33](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/8.md)).

The first group commenced the path but never grabbed the rod. They pressed forward — they were in motion in the right direction — but the rod was not in their hand. When the mist of darkness rose, they wandered off and were lost. They held no prescription, accepted no rest, made no proposal. There was nothing to hold.

The second group caught the rod, walked through the mist, came to the tree, and tasted the fruit. They had every posture the Brother of Jared held. But after they had partaken, they "did cast their eyes about as if they were ashamed... and they fell away into forbidden paths and were lost" ([1 Nephi 8:25, 28](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/8.md)). The mist did not stop them. The building did. Their failure was not at the start; it was at the end, after success had been reached. In October 2024, Elder David A. Bednar read this failure as a pride failure ([Bednar, *In the Space of Not Many Years*](../../gospel-library/eng/general-conference/2024/10/35bednar.md)). They had tasted the love of God and still fell, because pride took the eye off the rod and put it on a comparator. The comparator can take any form. It can be the building's external mockery. It can be the soul's own assessment of falling short. It can be a peer who appears to have arrived faster, with less effort, in a more impressive register. All three are the same structural failure. The eye has left the prototype.

The third group "did press their way forward, continually holding fast to the rod of iron, until they came forth and fell down and partook of the fruit of the tree" ([1 Nephi 8:30](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/8.md)). They held the prescription, rested in the grace already going forth, and proposed in humility. Two phrases differentiate them from the second group. They held *continually*. And they *fell down* before they partook. Humility at the tree, not just arrival at it. The Brother of Jared is the worked example of this third group. He held the prescription exactly. He accepted the steering as the Lord's. He climbed the mountain and brought the stones — and when the Lord's finger touched them, the Brother of Jared was not standing in pride at his proposal. He was so fully humbled that he could not be kept from seeing the One who touched them.

The fourth group was in the building from the start, mocking those who arrived. They never approached the rod. They never started the path. This book is not addressed to them.

The mist of darkness, importantly, falls on the third group too. Elder Holland in October 2009 made this correction explicit: the mist descends on all the travelers, the faithful as well as the ungrounded ones ([Holland, *Safety for the Soul*](../../gospel-library/eng/general-conference/2009/10/safety-for-the-soul.md)). The third group is not above the weather. The third group is recognizable by what their hand is on while they walk through it.

The pattern that takes a third-group soul into the second group is exactly the pattern Bednar traces in Helaman. Prosperity arrives. Pride enters — not into the church, but into the hearts of those who profess to belong to the church ([Helaman 3:33](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/3.md)). The hearts shift to vain things. The soul begins to boast in its own strength, and is left in its own strength ([Helaman 4:13](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/4.md)). The Spirit withdraws. In the space of not many years — Mormon uses the phrase three times across Helaman ([4:26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/4.md); [6:32](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/6.md); [7:6](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/7.md)) — a soul that held the discipline well becomes a soul going through the motions. The transformation is invisible from outside until it is not.

The corrective is the Lamanite posture. The Lamanites in Helaman are the unlikely heroes — less history, less institutional knowledge, less of the inheritance the Nephites took for granted. They rose while the Nephites fell because of two virtues. The first was "easiness and willingness to believe" ([Helaman 6:36](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/6.md)) — no hardened resistance, no insistence on understanding everything first, no requirement for signs. The second was "walking circumspectly" before God ([Helaman 15:5](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/15.md)) — Webster's 1828 sense of *cautiously, warily*. They watched their step. They did not assume they were safe.

The four groups are not types of people; they are postures available to any soul on any day. Most days a steward who has learned this is in the third group, holding continually. Some days they will catch themselves in the second group, the eye drifted, the discipline slackened. The work then is to grip again. The rod extends along the full length of the path. Wherever the hand falls when it reaches for the iron, the iron is there.

This is the ultimate geometry of hope. Hope is the anchor of the soul that "entereth into that within the veil" ([Hebrews 6:19](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/heb/6.md)). It keeps the gaze fixed on the Prototype. In Mormon's sermon, hope is the anticipation of seeing Him: "When he shall appear we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is; that we may have this hope; that we may be purified even as he is pure" ([Moroni 7:48](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/moro/7.md)). The Brother of Jared's eye was so single, and his faith so exceeding, that he could not be kept from seeing the One whose finger touched the stones. The anchor of hope, cast within the veil, pulled him through it. Face-to-face seership is the transition where hope is realized in sight, and the spirit-matter is fully quickened to abide the celestial glory.

## The Engineering Parallel

The engineer working with AI under the discipline of this book holds the same three postures the Brother of Jared held, and walks the same mist with the same four groups around them.

**Prescription** is the daily discipline not open to improvisation: the covenant on disk, the specification written before the code, the binding question asked before the prompt. These are the rate limits and API contracts of the soul. The senior engineer still writes the spec — not because they couldn't skip it, but because the prescription is what keeps the vessel watertight.

**Rest** is what is already in motion and not generated from the engineer's own strength: the training the model already carries, the behaviors that can be relied on, the judgment built over years. The Brother of Jared did not build a rudder; the engineer does not retrain the model. The work is to enter the vessel, use the tools that exist, and trust the wind already going forth.

**Proposal** is the engineer's own labor brought to the Master: the prompt that took time to refine, the context that took years to learn how to surface, the spec that anticipated the implicit constraints. The Brother of Jared climbed a high mountain and melted sixteen small stones out of a rock; the engineer climbs the long apprenticeship of learning a system before they can ask a useful question. The stones are the prepared mind; the Master's touch is what makes them shine in the darkness of an unknown problem.

The four groups describe how engineers hold these postures, or fail to. Group 1 is in motion without the rod — using AI with no spec, no covenant, no binding question — and gets lost in vague output. Group 4 is in the building, mocking: *covenants are LARP, specs are bureaucracy, just vibe-code and ship.* This book is not addressed to them. Group 3 holds the three postures continually: writes the spec on the project where they could skip it, watches the output even when it looks fine, and walks circumspectly precisely when the work is going well.

Group 2 is the chilling one. They learned the discipline, applied it, saw it work, shipped good projects under it — and then, in the space of not many weeks, dropped it. Not because it stopped working, but because prosperity bred pride, and pride moved the eye off the rod and onto a comparator: the team that ships faster, the idealized self not yet become, the peer in a more impressive register. Bednar's October 2024 diagnostic is the warning: the engineer most certain they are too disciplined to fall is the one already falling. The corrective is the Lamanite posture — the junior who expects to need the spec because they know they don't yet know, and who therefore watches their step. The senior holds that posture deliberately, because last week's good work does not keep their hand on the rod this morning. The mist falls on Group 3 too; walking through obscurity is not slipping. The verdict reads what the hand is on while it walks.

(For the fuller doctrinal treatment — the four groups, the pride mechanism, the two prepositions of hope — see [The Four Groups and the Engineer](https://cpuchip.net/studies/four-groups-and-the-engineer).)

Hope keeps the eye on the Prototype through all of it. But the seeing, like the softening, is not finally something the steward performs — and that limit is where the next chapter begins.

## Becoming Commitment

Because I recognize that my agency must operate across prescription, rest, and proposal to align my substance with eternal light, and that the eye must stay on the Prototype through prosperity and mist alike, I commit to:

1.  **Honor the prescriptions of safety.** I will respect the basic commandments and spiritual habits—daily scripture study, prayer, and repentance—that serve as the ventilation system of my soul, keeping the water of the world out and letting the Spirit in.
2.  **Rest in the winds of grace.** I will set down the anxiety of steering my own salvation. I will trust that the wind of Christ's Atonement has already gone forth and will carry my vessel through the deep. I will praise Him in the midst of the mountain waves.
3.  **Bring molten stones to the mount.** I will not ask the Lord to illuminate my path without bringing my own prepared substance. I will labor to refine my character, making my intentions transparent, and present my specific proposals to Him for His quickening touch.
4.  **Fix my gaze within the veil.** I will keep my eye single to the Prototype, anchoring my hope in the reality of Jesus Christ. I will look past the chaotic elements of the seashore and focus on the face that awaits me at the end of the voyage.
5.  **Walk circumspectly during prosperity.** I will watch my step most carefully in the seasons when the work is going well, recognizing that prosperity is what fans pride faster than poverty fans it. I will assume I could fall — especially when I am sure I cannot — and keep the daily grip on the rod whether the building is loud or silent.
