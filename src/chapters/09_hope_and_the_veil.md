# Chapter 9: Hope and the Veil

**Binding Question:** How does the "geometry of hope" function as an anchor to our souls when we are navigating darkness and the unknown?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And there were many whose faith was so exceedingly strong, even before Christ came, who could not be kept from within the veil, but truly saw with their eyes the things which they had beheld with an eye of faith, and they were glad."
> — [Ether 12:19](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.md)

---

## The Core Reframe

[Outline: In the scriptures, hope is not passive wishful thinking. It is an anchor of the soul (Hebrews 6:19), holding us fast to a reality we cannot yet see. Hope has a directional geometry—it reaches within the veil. In the story of the Brother of Jared (Ether 2-3), he faced three problems: no light, no steering, no air. The Lord solved the air and steering, but left the light to him. His hope led him to molten sixteen small stones and present them to the Lord, which pierced the veil.]

---

## The Engineering Parallel

[Outline: This geometry maps to how we navigate complex system designs. We often face projects with zero visibility (no light) and deep uncertainty. We cannot see the final solution. Hope is the act of formulating a precise hypothesis (the stones) and presenting it to the execution engine. By committing to a design and executing a prototype, we pierce the veil of complexity, turning speculative hope into verified reality.]

---

## Becoming Commitment

[Outline: When facing complex, ambiguous problems in my calling or engineering work, I will not sit in passive confusion. I will build a concrete proposal—my sixteen stones—and present it to the Lord and my councils, acting in faith.]
