# Chapter 9: Hope and the Veil

**Binding Question:** How do the three stewardship patterns of the Brother of Jared (Prescription, Rest, and Proposal) and the geometry of Hope guide a steward's active agency?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And behold, we have seen in this record that one of these was the brother of Jared; for so great was his faith in God, that when God put forth his finger he could not hide it from the sight of the brother of Jared, because of his word which he had spoken unto him, which word he had obtained by faith."
> — [Ether 12:20](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.md) [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.md?verse=20)

## The Core Reframe

Sanctification is not an abstract forensic transaction; it is a physical and relational reality. The process of becoming like God requires a structural reorganization of our substance, driven by the Lord's grace and our active agency, and anchored by our attentional focus. This relationship is patterned in the Brother of Jared's three engineering problems—air, steering, and light—which map to three distinct stewardship structures: Prescription, Rest, and Proposal.

The first problem was air. Without it, the occupants of the barges would suffocate in the closed vessels. The Lord's solution was a strict, non-negotiable prescription: "Behold, thou shalt make a hole in the top, and also in the bottom; and when thou shalt suffer for air thou shalt unstop the hole and receive air. And if it be so that the water come in upon thee, behold, ye shall stop the hole, that ye may not perish in the flood" ([Ether 2:20](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/2.md)). In the physics of refinement, this prescription represents the outer boundaries of celestial law. Under Doctrine and Covenants 88:34, "that which is governed by law is also preserved by law." The barges, built "tight like unto a dish," represent the soul prepared to withstand the chaotic, heavy elements of a fallen world. But a sealed vessel cannot survive without ventilation; it requires a structured inlet for the breath of life (the Spirit) and a quick mechanism to close it when the elements threaten to drown it. The prescription—drill here, open now, close then—is the daily discipline of the commandments. It is not open to steward improvisation. When a steward follows the prescription exactly, the structural integrity of the vessel is preserved.

The second problem was steering. The barges had no helm, rudder, or sails, yet the Brother of Jared did not ask about steering in his second prayer. The Lord addressed it anyway: "For behold, ye shall be as a whale in the midst of the sea; for the mountain waves shall dash upon you. Nevertheless, I will bring you up again out of the depths of the sea; for the winds have gone forth out of my mouth, and also the rains and the floods have I sent forth" ([Ether 2:24](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/2.md)). The wind that drove the barges across the ocean was already going forth before the steward boarded. In the mechanics of refinement, this is the atoning grace of Christ's resurrection—the passive, constant pull that quickens the universe and draws the soul home. The steward cannot build a rudder to navigate the infinite ocean of eternity; the navigation belongs entirely to the Lord. The steward's part is to enter the vessel, rest in this established grace, and sing praises. This is the geometry of hope. Because the wind is already blowing toward the promised land, hope is not wishful thinking but a secure anchor. Hope "maketh not ashamed" because the power driving the vessel is the Master's breath.

The third problem was light. The Lord refused to prescribe an answer, instead asking: "What will ye that I should do that ye may have light in your vessels?" ([Ether 2:23](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/2.md)). He constrained the question by ruling out windows and fire, then threw it back to the steward. The Brother of Jared responded by climbing a high mountain, molting sixteen small stones out of a rock, and presenting them to the Lord: "touch these stones, O Lord, with thy finger, and prepare them that they may shine forth in darkness; and they shall shine forth unto us in the vessels which we have prepared, that we may have light while we shall cross the sea" ([Ether 3:4](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/3.md)). In the physics of refinement, these stones represent the fine matter of the human spirit. Opaque rock cannot transmit light. To receive the light of truth, the steward must labor, molting the stones until they are "white and clear, even as transparent glass" ([Ether 3:1](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/3.md)). This is the work of repentance and sanctification: purging the dross of sin to make our spirit-matter transparent to the glory of God.

The quickening occurs when the Master touches the stones. The finger of the Lord was a physical reality—the Brother of Jared saw it "like unto flesh and blood" ([Ether 3:6](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/ether/3.md)). Christ's physical reality—His pre-mortal spirit body and His subsequent mortal descent below all things—established the physical circuit through which this purifying power is conducted. The proposal pattern is how active agency brings its refined substance to the Master, allowing Him to touch and quicken what we have prepared. 

This sight is the ultimate geometry of hope. Hope is the anchor of the soul that "entereth into that within the veil" ([Hebrews 6:19](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/heb/6.md)). It keeps the gaze fixed on the Prototype (Christ). In Mormon's sermon, hope is the anticipation of seeing Him: "When he shall appear we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is; that we may have this hope; that we may be purified even as he is pure" ([Moroni 7:48](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/moro/7.md)). When the eye is single to God's glory, the whole body is filled with light and comprehends all things. The Brother of Jared's eye was so single, and his faith so exceeding, that he could not be kept from seeing. The anchor of hope, cast within the veil, pulled him through it. Face-to-face seership is the transition where hope is realized in sight, and the spirit-matter is fully quickened to abide the celestial glory.

## The Engineering Parallel

We see this same three-tier structure in modern system design. 

In a cloud infrastructure, **Prescription** is the declarative configuration file (like a Dockerfile or a Terraform spec). It defines the non-negotiable boundaries of the system—which ports are open, which volumes are mounted, and what memory limits are enforced. The engineer does not improvise on these basic laws; if the configuration violates the network protocol, the container suffocates and fails to launch. Following the prescription exactly preserves the basic safety of the system.

Next is **Rest**. Once the infrastructure is declared, the scheduler (like Kubernetes or a background execution engine) takes over. The engineer does not write the low-level code to route packets or schedule CPU cycles; that "wind" is already blowing. The system automatically maintains state, restarts crashed pods, and routes traffic. The engineer rests in this automated runtime, trusting that the established platform will carry the workload to its destination.

Finally, we have **Proposal**. When the system must solve a novel problem—such as generating a complex query or optimizing an AI agent's inference loop—there is no pre-written prescription. The engineer must act as a steward, collecting the raw data (the stones), formatting it into a clean prompt template, and presenting it to the model. The touch of the model (glory) transforms the raw input into active, illuminated instruction. The proposal pattern is the creative boundary of the engineer's agency, where raw material is presented for intelligence to touch.

## Becoming Commitment

Because I recognize that my agency must operate across prescription, rest, and proposal to align my substance with eternal light, I commit to:

1.  **Honor the prescriptions of safety.** I will respect the basic commandments and spiritual habits—daily scripture study, prayer, and repentance—that serve as the ventilation system of my soul, keeping the water of the world out and letting the Spirit in.
2.  **Rest in the winds of grace.** I will set down the anxiety of steering my own salvation. I will trust that the wind of Christ's Atonement has already gone forth and will carry my vessel through the deep. I will praise Him in the midst of the mountain waves.
3.  **Bring molten stones to the mount.** I will not ask the Lord to illuminate my path without bringing my own prepared substance. I will labor to refine my character, making my intentions transparent, and present my specific proposals to Him for His quickening touch.
4.  **Fix my gaze within the veil.** I will keep my eye single to the Prototype, anchoring my hope in the reality of Jesus Christ. I will look past the chaotic elements of the seashore and focus on the face that awaits me at the end of the voyage.
