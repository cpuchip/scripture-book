# Practice 1 · Talk, Don't Command

In October 2025 I sat down to build a video game with my kid. I had no design. I didn't even have a clear question. What I had was a direction, *something the two of us could make together,* and an empty folder.

So I didn't write a specification, and I didn't hand down an order. I typed something close to this: *let's set up a notes folder and a plan; ask me some questions to get one game put together to start with.*

The model came back with fourteen questions. What kind of game? How many players? On what device? What does winning look like? I answered them one at a time, and somewhere in the answering, the vague *something* grew edges. It became a thing we could actually build. The conversation hadn't decorated a plan I already had. The conversation was how the plan got written.

It took me years to notice: **you do not start with the answer. You start with a vision, and the conversation is how you find it.**

Sometimes the vision is concrete. A year later I woke up from a dream with a whole system in my head: a way to hand work to AI agents under rules and watch them run. I could see it working before it existed. When the vision is that clear, the conversation is short: I describe what I see, we blueprint it, I press on the blueprint with questions until it holds, and we build. That system was running real work in three days and stood up in about three weeks.

But more often the vision is vague. I have a direction and a feeling, not a picture — the game with my kid, or the book you are holding. Then the conversation is longer, and its entire job is to *sharpen*: to turn the feeling into something with edges. Questions do the sharpening. Sometimes I ask them. More often, when I cannot see the shape myself, I ask the model to ask *me*.

In both cases, the conversation is not the absence of a plan. It is how the plan gets made. Whether I saw the vision whole or talked my way into seeing it, the talking comes first. The plan is born in the conversation, not skipped by it.

The opposite of talking is commanding. Commanding is dropping a half-formed order into the box, *build me a dashboard, write the tests,* and accepting the first plausible thing that comes back. It feels faster. It isn't. A command from a fuzzy vision earns you a confident answer to a question you never finished asking, and you spend the next hour finding out you didn't mean any of it. What you bring is what you get back: bring fog, and the model hands you a confident, polished version of your own confusion.

Today the practice looks roughly like this, and it will look different by the time you read it. An idea arrives, vague or concrete. We talk. If it's concrete, we blueprint it on the spot and ratify it by walking a few phases as questions. If it's vague, we counsel first, talking until the vision has edges, and only then blueprint and phase it. Either way I set the model loose on a phase, and I watch where it goes.

The tools will turn over. The models will get stronger, the editor smarter; the word *prompt* may not survive the decade. None of that touches the shape underneath: creation begins in counsel, the vision precedes the building, and the fastest road to something worth building is to talk your way to seeing it clearly first.

That shape is not new, and it is not mine. Before the world was made, "the Gods took counsel among themselves" ([Abraham 4:26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md)) and only then went down to form it. The spiritual creation came before the physical one ([Moses 3:5](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/moses/3.md)). The thing was envisioned in full before a particle of it was moved. The conversation that finds your vision is the small, daily shadow of the counsel that precedes all creation. (Part Two: Intent · Spiritual Creation — the doctrine under this practice.)

**Try this.** The next time you sit down to build with AI and you don't quite know what you're building, don't command it. Give it the direction, then say: *ask me questions until we both know what this is.* Answer them honestly. Watch the vision grow edges. Then build.

> **Remember:** You don't start with the answer — you start with a vision, and the conversation is how you find it. See it whole, and you blueprint it. Can't see it yet, and you talk until you can. Either way, the talk is the plan being born, not the plan being skipped.
