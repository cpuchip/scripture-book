# Chapter 6: Bilateral Covenant

**Binding Question:** Why does bilateral commitment produce greater alignment and sounder execution than unilateral commands?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say; but when ye do not what I say, ye have no promise."
> — [Doctrine and Covenants 82:10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/82.md)

---

## The Core Reframe

[Outline: God does not govern by arbitrary force; He governs by covenant. A covenant is a bilateral binding agreement. "I, the Lord, am bound..." represents the divine willingness to be governed by law. When we keep the law, the outcome is guaranteed. Covenants create predictability, trust, and structural integrity. In our relationships and creations, unilateral command produces resistance and compliance flatness, whereas bilateral covenants invite active engagement and agency.]

---

## The Engineering Parallel

[Outline: We see this in human-AI collaboration. Traditional instructions are unilateral prompts. But when we establish a bilateral "covenant" document (like `covenant.yaml` in this project), specifying what the human commits to (reading outputs, providing binding questions, flagging errors) and what the agent commits to (verifying quotes, checking existing work, updating memory), the collaboration stabilizes. It moves from transactional commands to a trusted partnership governed by shared rules.]

---

## Becoming Commitment

[Outline: I commit to honor my side of the covenant by reading all outputs fully and never bypassing the review process. I will establish clear, mutual expectations in all my collaborative undertakings, both human and artificial.]
