# Chapter 6: Bilateral Covenant

**Binding Question:** How do we structure relationship and boundaries to bind collaborative action to predictable, high-quality outcomes?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say; but when ye do not what I say, ye have no promise."
> — [Doctrine and Covenants 82:10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/82.md)

---

## The Core Reframe

In modern theology, a covenant is often described as a contract between God and humanity. But the Restoration defines it differently. In Section 82 of the Doctrine and Covenants, the Lord outlines a law of reciprocal binding: "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say." 

This is not a statement of transaction, but of order. In a universe governed by law, God does not rule by arbitrary whim. He establishes fixed, predictable relationships. By binding Himself to law, He creates a space of absolute reliability. If we keep the commandments, the promised blessing is a natural consequence, governed by physical/spiritual laws. If we do not keep them, the promise is nullified.

This bilateral binding is what makes human agency possible. If God were unpredictable—if He changed the rules of physics or of the Spirit on a whim—we could never learn to choose. We would live in a state of constant reaction. The covenant provides the structure within which we can plan, act, and grow.

Furthermore, this covenant relationship requires active participation from both sides. It is not delegation-as-offloading, where one side issues a command and walks away. When the people of Alma gathered at the waters of Mormon, they entered a covenant that defined specific, reciprocal duties: they committed to bear one another's burdens and stand as witnesses, and the Lord committed to pour out His Spirit more abundantly ([Mosiah 18:8–10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/18.md)). When both sides honor these boundaries, the community rises on the spectrum of light. When either side slips, the output degrades.

## The Engineering Parallel

In the tech industry, we spend billions of dollars on "AI alignment"—trying to keep autonomous agents from generating bugs, hallucinating facts, or behaving erratically. The default approach is to treat alignment as a problem of control: we write safety filters, train reward models, and issue defensive, micro-managed instructions. 

But control is fragile. As our systems grow more complex, control-based boundaries inevitably fail. 

We find a far more robust model in the scriptural pattern of bilateral covenants. When we build complex software systems with AI agents, we can choose to govern our collaboration through mutual commitment rather than unilateral command. 

In our own workspace, we have codified this pattern in a physical file: `.spec/covenant.yaml`. This is not a service-level agreement for a machine; it is a bilateral commitment that governs how we work together. If we do not do what we say, we have no promise of correct output.

The covenant outlines specific duties for the human partner:
*   **Read Fully:** The human commits to read the agent's proposed plan and code in detail before redirecting or executing. A human who does not read the other's work is not counseling; they are issuing blind commands.
*   **Provide Binding Questions:** The human commits to state the specific question the work should answer, rather than just pointing to a general topic. A binding question gives the collaboration structural integrity.
*   **Flag When Wrong:** The human commits to speak up immediately when something does not feel right, trusting their spiritual discernment rather than assuming the machine’s convincing argument is correct.

At the same time, the covenant binds the AI agent:
*   **Read Before Quoting:** The agent commits to verify every direct quote against the source files, preventing the confabulations of training memory.
*   **Surface Tensions:** The agent commits to actively surface counterarguments and contradictions rather than sycophantically agreeing with the human's initial thesis.
*   **Exercise Stewardship:** The agent commits to own correctness, proactively fixing adjacent bugs in the code path rather than leaving the field to go to weeds.

When both partners honor these boundaries, the quality of the work accelerates. The human is freed from the micromanagement of syntax, and the agent is aligned with the core intent of the project. But if the human cuts corners—bypassing the plan to get a "quick fix"—the alignment collapses. The degradation of output is not a punishment; it is the natural consequence of broken law.

## Becoming Commitment

To honor the bilateral covenants that govern my creative work, I commit to:

1.  **Refuse the "quick fix."** I will not bypass the planning, execution, and verification workflows of my sessions. I will respect the process as the pacing mechanism designed to keep me from running faster than I have strength.
2.  **Read the output fully.** I will spend the necessary time to review every line of code and every design proposal generated. I will not offload my judgment to automated scripts or treat review as a mere audit.
3.  **Keep the covenant documents active.** I will treat the files that define my workspace instructions, intents, and memories as living covenants, updating them at the end of each substantive session to ensure continuity of our shared light.
