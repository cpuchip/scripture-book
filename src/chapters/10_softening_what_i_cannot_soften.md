# Chapter 10: Softening What I Cannot Soften

*Atonement · Yielding*

**Binding Question:** How does a person receive a softened heart when scripture indicates we cannot soften it ourselves?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And therefore, he that will harden his heart, the same receiveth the lesser portion of the word; and he that will not harden his heart, to him is given the greater portion of the word, until it is given unto him to know the mysteries of God until he know them in full."
> — [Alma 12:10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/12.md?verse=10) [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/12.md?verse=10)

---

## The Core Reframe

Restoration scripture cuts down the self-improvement fantasy of engineering our own hearts. Nephi's classic confession of conversion outlines a strict division of labor: "And it came to pass that I, Nephi, being exceedingly young, nevertheless being large in stature, and also having great desires to know of the mysteries of God, wherefore, I did cry unto the Lord; and behold he did visit me, and did soften my heart that I did believe all the words which had been spoken by my father; wherefore, I did not rebel against him like unto my brothers" ([1 Nephi 2:16](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/2.md?verse=16)). [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/2.md?verse=16) Nephi did not perform a self-directed heart transplant. He brought diligent desires, lowliness, and a cry, but the visiting and the softening remained the exclusive operations of the Lord. The human steward is the recipient of the change, not its author.

If the softening of the heart is a divine act, the human variable is defined in the negative. Alma teaches that our agency-bearing choice is not to make ourselves soft, but to refuse to harden ourselves: "he that will not harden his heart, to him is given the greater portion of the word" ([Alma 12:10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/12.md?verse=10)). Hardening is the active resistance of the human will—the high hand of rebellion that locks the channel against the light. Softening is the work of grace that flows automatically into the soul when that resistance ceases. Our primary task is to identify and halt the specific habits, distractions, and idols that calcify the heart, allowing the Lord to perform the cleansing.

The Lord's exclusive ownership of transformation is declared through the prophets. In Ezekiel, the Lord claims all the active verbs of change: "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh" ([Ezekiel 36:25–26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/ezek/36.md?verse=25-26)). [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/ezek/36.md?verse=25-26) The human role is simply to yield the vessel and put down the idols. When Lamoni's father was taught the plan of redemption, he stopped trying to trade external possessions like his kingdom. Instead, he prostrated himself and offered the ultimate currency: "I will give away all my sins to know thee" ([Alma 22:18](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/22.md?verse=18)). This trade is simultaneous; we give away our hardness, and the Lord returns a soft heart.

This yielding is both a sudden surrender and an accumulating discipline. Helaman records the slow work of refinement: "Nevertheless they did fast and pray oft, and did wax stronger and stronger in their humility, and firmer and firmer in the faith of Christ, unto the filling their souls with joy and consolation, yea, even to the purifying and the sanctification of their hearts, which sanctification cometh because of their yielding their hearts unto God" ([Helaman 3:35](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/3.md?verse=35)). [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/3.md?verse=35) This progressive softening requires us to face the painful dismantling of our self-will. As Job observed, "For God maketh my heart soft, and the Almighty troubleth me" ([Job 23:16](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/job/23.md?verse=16)). The troubling and the softening are two names for the same event. We must allow the Lord to trouble our comfortable defaults, letting Him break down our defensive walls so that He can rebuild us in His image.

## The Engineering Parallel

Scripture has carried the asymmetry of softening for millennia. Two engineering versions of it sit close to the engineer's daily work — one in the older agricultural register, one in the newer prompting loop.

The older one is gardening, and it is already in scripture. Mark 4, the parable of the growing seed: the man casts the seed, sleeps, rises night and day; "the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how" ([Mark 4:27](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/mark/4.md?verse=27)). [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/mark/4.md?verse=27) Alma 32: the soul plants the word, nourishes it with diligence and patience, and the tree grows — but the growing itself belongs to the seed. The gardener's part is clearing stones, removing weeds, watering, and waiting. The growing is exclusive to the seed and the soil. A gardener who tries to make the seed sprout by squeezing it harder breaks it. A gardener who keeps the soil clear gets a tree.

The newer one is the engineer working with AI under the discipline. The AI returns output, and the output is "wrong." Too literal. Missing a constraint. Optimizing for the wrong thing. The reflex is to assume the AI was the problem — better prompting, more context, sharper instructions, a stronger model.

But the work of refinement is the opposite reflex. The wrongness in the output very often reveals where the engineer's intent was vague. The constraint the AI missed was the constraint the engineer never quite named. The framing the AI optimized for was the framing the engineer left implicit. The AI is producing exactly what the input was: a faithful reflection of the unspoken assumptions in the prompt. The "wrong" output is a mirror.

A smarter model rarely closes the gap. What you can yield is your own assumed-clarity. The engineer's part is the same part the gardener has: release the *I already know what I want* stance and let the surface surprise you. The mirror shows what you actually asked, not what you thought you asked. The yielding is the engineer's labor; the surfacing of the gap is the AI's contribution. The softening is what both leave room for.

This is the same shape as the heart's softening that the chapter has been describing. The soul cannot soften itself. But the soul can yield its hardness — the assumed self-sufficiency, the certainty it already knows, the closed posture toward correction. When the hardness yields, the Lord visits. The Spirit's part is the gift. The soul's part is the room. Where the room is made, the gift arrives. Where the room is held closed, no gift can enter.

Again the rhyme holds and the substance parts. A vague prompt revealing itself in a wrong output is a small instance of an older mechanism. The AI is not the Spirit. The engineer's yielding of "I already know what I want" is not the soul's yielding of hardness against the Lord. But the structural pattern is the same: release the closed posture, and what was waiting can arrive. One Author drew both motions; only one of them softens a heart.

## Becoming Commitment

Because I recognize that I cannot soften my own heart, but must yield my agency to invite the Lord's transformation, I commit to:

1.  **Practice the morning bow.** I will start each day by physically kneeling to offer the prayer of Lamoni's father: *wilt thou make thyself known unto me, and I will give away all my sins to know thee*. I will surrender the meta-sin of trying to manage my own spiritual progress, placing my day completely in His hands.
2.  **Redirect the first hour.** I am working toward protecting the first hour of my morning from digital inputs—no social media, news, or work notifications until I have spent time in scripture and silent reflection. I keep it less often than I want; naming it as the aim is how I keep reaching for it, refusing to let the scroll harden my heart before the day begins.
3.  **Identify my hardening pathways.** I will pay attention to the moments when I feel defensive, distracted, or cynical. Rather than trying to force myself to feel soft, I will stop, identify the specific pride or anxiety that is blocking the channel, and explicitly yield it to the Savior.
4.  **Yield to the troubling.** When my plans are disrupted or my character is challenged, I will not rebel. I will recognize the troubling of my heart as the beginning of the Lord's softening work, allowing Him to dismantle my self-will to make room for His grace.
