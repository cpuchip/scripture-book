# Chapter 10: Softening What I Cannot Soften

**Binding Question:** How does a person receive a softened heart when scripture indicates we cannot soften it ourselves?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And therefore, he that will harden his heart, the same receiveth the lesser portion of the word; and he that will not harden his heart, to him is given the greater portion of the word, until it is given unto him to know the mysteries of God until he know them in full."
> — [Alma 12:10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/12.md) [qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/12.md?verse=10)

---

## The Core Reframe

Restoration scripture cuts down the self-improvement fantasy of engineering our own hearts. Nephi's classic confession of conversion outlines a strict division of labor: "And it came to pass that I, Nephi, being exceedingly young, nevertheless being large in stature, and also having great desires to know of the mysteries of God, wherefore, I did cry unto the Lord; and behold he did visit me, and did soften my heart that I did believe all the words which had been spoken by my father; wherefore, I did not rebel against him like unto my brothers" ([1 Nephi 2:16](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/2.md)). Nephi did not perform a self-directed heart transplant. He brought diligent desires, lowliness, and a cry, but the visiting and the softening remained the exclusive operations of the Lord. The human steward is the recipient of the change, not its author.

If the softening of the heart is a divine act, the human variable is defined in the negative. Alma teaches that our agency-bearing choice is not to make ourselves soft, but to refuse to harden ourselves: "he that will not harden his heart, to him is given the greater portion of the word" ([Alma 12:10](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/12.md)). Hardening is the active resistance of the human will—the high hand of rebellion that locks the channel against the light. Softening is the work of grace that flows automatically into the soul when that resistance ceases. Our primary task is to identify and halt the specific habits, distractions, and idols that calcify the heart, allowing the Lord to perform the cleansing.

The Lord's exclusive ownership of transformation is declared through the prophets. In Ezekiel, the Lord claims all the active verbs of change: "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh" ([Ezekiel 36:25–26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/ezek/36.md)). The human role is simply to yield the vessel and put down the idols. When Lamoni's father was taught the plan of redemption, he stopped trying to trade external possessions like his kingdom. Instead, he prostrated himself and offered the ultimate currency: "I will give away all my sins to know thee" ([Alma 22:18](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/alma/22.md)). This trade is simultaneous; we give away our hardness, and the Lord returns a soft heart.

This yielding is both a sudden surrender and an accumulating discipline. Helaman records the slow work of refinement: "Nevertheless they did fast and pray oft, and did wax stronger and stronger in their humility, and firmer and firmer in the faith of Christ, unto the filling their souls with joy and consolation, yea, even to the purifying and the sanctification of their hearts, which sanctification cometh because of their yielding their hearts unto God" ([Helaman 3:35](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/hel/3.md)). This progressive softening requires us to face the painful dismantling of our self-will. As Job observed, "For God maketh my heart soft, and the Almighty troubleth me" ([Job 23:16](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/job/23.md)). The troubling and the softening are two names for the same event. We must allow the Lord to trouble our comfortable defaults, letting Him break down our defensive walls so that He can rebuild us in His image.

## The Engineering Parallel

In software development, this asymmetry of agency is mirrored in connection pooling and cache management. An engineer cannot force a clogged or deadlocked network socket to open by writing raw optimization logic; the socket is controlled by the operating system kernel. When connection pools exhaust and the system stalls, the engineer's task is not to rewrite the socket code. The developer must identify the leaks—the unreleased locks, the runaway loops, and the stale resources that are hogging the database threads. The engineer yields the resource by calling the close method, releasing the lock, and clearing the channel. Once the blockages are removed, the operating system naturally restores the socket and refreshes the connection pool.

Similarly, an AI agent's performance cannot be improved by attempting to hack the underlying transformer model. The developer cannot directly adjust the billion-weight parameters of the neural network during execution. If the agent gets stuck in a loop of repetitive, low-value outputs, the engineer's part is to clear the context window, prune the bad history, and adjust the system prompt filters. The developer does not write the reasoning engine; he simply releases the constraints that cause the agent to harden its attention. By cleaning the input pipeline, the engineer invites the model's natural capability to flow through, returning clean and optimized results.

## Becoming Commitment

Because I recognize that I cannot soften my own heart, but must yield my agency to invite the Lord's transformation, I commit to:

1.  **Practice the morning bow.** I will start each day by physically kneeling to offer the prayer of Lamoni's father: *wilt thou make thyself known unto me, and I will give away all my sins to know thee*. I will surrender the meta-sin of trying to manage my own spiritual progress, placing my day completely in His hands.
2.  **Redirect the first hour.** I will protect the first hour of my morning from digital inputs. I will not look at social media, news, or work notifications until I have spent time in scripture and silent reflection, refusing to allow the scroll to harden my heart before the day begins.
3.  **Identify my hardening pathways.** I will pay attention to the moments when I feel defensive, distracted, or cynical. Rather than trying to force myself to feel soft, I will stop, identify the specific pride or anxiety that is blocking the channel, and explicitly yield it to the Savior.
4.  **Yield to the troubling.** When my plans are disrupted or my character is challenged, I will not rebel. I will recognize the troubling of my heart as the beginning of the Lord's softening work, allowing Him to dismantle my self-will to make room for His grace.
