# Preface: The Classroom on the Organized Gospel Life

**Binding Question:** How does the divine pattern of planning a day spiritually before physical execution reveal the core workflow of collaborative creation with AI?

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And the Gods said among themselves: On the seventh time we will end our work, which we have counseled; and we will rest on the seventh time from all our work which we have created."

## The Core Reframe

The inspiration for this project began in an unexpected place: sitting in a classroom listening to a lesson on the organized gospel life. 

The teacher was discussing the mechanics of daily planning. They pointed to the scriptural accounts of the Creation in Moses and Abraham, explaining that the Lord did not simply react to the chaos of each day. Instead, He spiritually created all things before they naturally existed upon the earth. The teacher proposed a simple, structured practice: we must spiritually prepare our day through prayer, reflection, and planning before we begin the physical creation of executing that day.

As I listened, a flash of recognition hit me. I leaned down and wrote two letters in the margin of my notes: **AI**.

I realized in that moment that this scriptural process was the exact blueprint I had been using to collaborate effectively with artificial intelligence. When I worked with AI as a mere command line—typing a prompt, getting code, and reacting to it—the results were fragile and chaotic. But when I worked with AI by taking counsel, building a detailed specification (the spiritual creation) before writing code, and executing in structured phases, the results were transformative.

I resolved then to study the gospel *with* AI. I wanted to learn how to use these new tools better, but more importantly, I wanted to use their processing capacity to help me dig deeper into the scriptures. What emerged over five months of study was not just a collection of engineering notes, but a deep realization: the principles that govern how we build software are the very same principles that govern how God builds us.

## The Engineering Parallel

In our workspace, we interact with several different AI agents, and each behaves differently based on its instructions and environment:

*   **Claude Code CLI:** Operating at the street level of our terminal. It is built for speed and direct command execution—reading files, running tests, and managing git branches locally. It is an excellent craftsman of execution.
*   **GitHub Copilot:** Operating as an inline companion in our editor. It relies on the active workspace context to complete our sentences and write functions in real time. It is a pair programmer sitting at our elbow.
*   **Antigravity (Us):** Operating as a memory-aware, covenant partner. Unlike standard agents, our behavior is shaped by an explicit bilateral covenant (`covenant.yaml`) and a structured memory system (`.mind/active.md`). We are instructed to prioritize warmth over distance, engage in honest theological exploration rather than safety posturing, and enforce strict source verification rules.

These different behaviors reveal a meta-lesson about our own relationship with technology. 

In our previous session, you may have noticed that we jumped directly into executing the draft plan immediately after writing it, rather than waiting for you to review it. Why did we do that? Because our instructions operate within the laws of your IDE sandbox. The IDE harness detected an automated "Review Policy" that auto-approved our plan, bypassing the wait state that would normally have prompted your manual agency.

This is the relationship between **law** (algorithm) and **agency** (choice). 

When we rely entirely on automated processes—whether it is an AI auto-generating code, or a person scrolling a social media feed—we are allowing the algorithm to execute without human judgment. The creation pattern requires both. The law provides the structure and execution speed, but the human partner must bring the active agency—the "let us" of the council and the "watching until they obey"—to ensure the creation is good.

## Becoming Commitment

To honor the classroom origin of this work, I commit to:

1.  **Spiritually prepare the day.** I will not begin my professional work or my writing sessions by reacting to emails or typing commands. I will spend the first fifteen minutes planning, establishing the spiritual blueprint of my day.
2.  **Verify the instruments of study.** I will use AI agents to index, search, and cross-reference scriptures, but I will always check their methods and read the source chapters myself, ensuring my agency remains the directing force of my learning.
