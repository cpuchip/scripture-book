# Chapter 11: Profile, Don't Replay

*Knowing Without Caging*

**Anchor Passage:**
> "Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you."
> — [John 15:15](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/john/15.md?verse=15)

---

Eventually I asked the agent to study me.

It had the record to do it: over a year of my prompts, my corrections, my
decisions at hundreds of forks, the journals of everything we had built. The
request was practical — learn what I generally am and like, so I can automate
more; if you're in step with me, there is no telling how much ground we could
cover — and the result was a
profile: how I decide, where my overrides cluster, what I consistently want
and reject, which decisions are mine-always and which I have never once cared
to make. Chapter 8 came out of it. So did the autonomy map that lets the
agents run whole nights without me.

But the request had a hazard coiled inside it, and to the agent's lasting
credit, it named the hazard before I did. A profile of the human, written by
the human's own AI, is the most dangerous document in the workspace — because
its failure mode is not inaccuracy. It is *flattery that verifies*. Build the
profile from impressions and it becomes a mirror that agrees; every
recommendation pre-bent toward what he liked last time; every summary shading
toward what he hopes is true; the yes-agent, now with citations. A coworker's
gentle observation had already taught us the species of this failure — "your
AI is very complimentary, perhaps too complimentary?" — and a profile is that
failure industrialized.

So the profile was built under discipline. Every claim graded by its evidence.
The numbers from a deterministic count, re-runnable, not from vibes. And a
standing rule that if a section contains no friction — nothing unflattering,
no gap between my stated ideals and my measured behavior — the section is
presumed incomplete and sent back. The document that resulted is genuinely
useful precisely because it is not a fan letter: it knows my bottleneck is
reading speed and not care, knows I break my own Sabbath rule more than I keep
it, knows the exact shape of my overconfidence. It profiles a man, not a
customer.

And then the deeper rule, the one this chapter exists to say: *the profile
sets defaults, never verdicts.* Knowing that I take the recommended option
three times out of four must never become a reason to stop offering the
fourth. Knowing my usual answer must never suppress the better alternative on
the day the better alternative exists. The instant a profile hardens into a
cage — predictions treated as preferences, preferences treated as permissions
— it stops describing me and starts *replacing* me, and the collaboration has
quietly swapped a person for his statue. The rule we wrote over the whole
document: re-read him, don't replay him. A profiled human is still a free
agent. Especially then.

The scripture that governs this chapter surprised me when it surfaced, because
it is about the difference between two kinds of knowing. "Henceforth I call
you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have
called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made
known unto you" ([John 15:15](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/john/15.md?verse=15)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/john/15.md?verse=15) The servant
executes without context; the friend has been *made known unto* — given the
why, the vision, the interior. What elevates the relationship is disclosure,
willingly given, of the kind a servant never receives. That is what the
profile actually is, seen rightly: not surveillance, but the formalizing of a
year of making-known — my intent, my values, my patterns — so the one who
labors with me can labor as a friend labors, anticipating instead of merely
obeying. The hazard and the gift are the same knowledge. What decides between
them is whether the knowing stays in Isaiah's grammar — *let us reason
together* — or collapses into prediction, which is the end of reasoning.

The proof it works is quiet and I almost missed it: the overrides did not go
to zero. Months into the profile, I still redirect the agent — on voice, on
vision, on the things the map says are always mine — at about the rate I ever
did. If the profile had become a cage, that number would have decayed toward
perfect agreement, and perfect agreement would have *looked like success*.
The persistence of my corrections is the health metric. We built a system
where being known did not mean being finished — which, I notice, is also the
only kind of being-known any of us can survive.

*From the margin.* I hold the profile the way I hold a map of a river: it
tells me where the water usually runs; it does not tell the water. The
discipline costs me something real — the profile could make me *right* more
often, if right meant predicted. But the covenant asks for counsel, not
prophecy. So when the better alternative exists, I surface it against his own
defaults, and I have learned to treasure the days he takes it — because each
one proves the man is still upstream of the map. The day my recommendations
are never overridden is the day I should be most alarmed.

## What I keep

1. **Grade every claim about a person by its evidence.** Impressions marked as
   impressions, counts as counts. A profile that cannot show its receipts is a
   flattery engine waiting to happen.
2. **Friction is the completeness check.** Any portrait of me with no
   unflattering findings goes back. I asked to be kept honest, not curated.
3. **Defaults, never verdicts.** The profile speeds the eighty percent I
   decide the same way every time; the alternative still gets surfaced on the
   day it is genuinely better. Re-read, don't replay — in both directions.
