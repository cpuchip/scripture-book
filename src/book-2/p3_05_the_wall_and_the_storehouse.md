# Chapter 5: The Wall and the Storehouse

*Stewardship · Consecration*

**Anchor Passage:**
> "For it is expedient that I, the Lord, should make every man accountable, as a steward over earthly blessings, which I have made and prepared for my creatures."
> — [Doctrine and Covenants 104:13](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/104.md?verse=13)

---

The day came when our substrate had to grow walls. More than one steward would
share it: some work held in common, some held private, some opened by explicit
grant. The industry has a name for this — multi-tenancy with enterprise
security — and an instinct to go with the name: lock it down, because every
other user is a threat until proven otherwise, and the measure of a good system
is how little it trusts. Before writing a single access policy, we stopped and
asked whether the Lord has a different instinct about walls.

He does, and the difference begins with the noun. The world's word is *tenant*
— a renter, a stranger paying for space. The Lord's economy does not begin with
property at all; it begins with a person made answerable for property:
"organize yourselves and appoint every man his stewardship; That every man may
give an account unto me"
([D&C 104:11–12](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/104.md?verse=11-12)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/104.md?verse=11-12)
Read that as an architect and the data model is already drawn: the owner column
on a row is not a tenant key, it is an *account*-ability — a name the system
can call to render an account. And the steward is no prisoner of his plot: "I
prepared all things, and have given unto the children of men to be agents unto
themselves" (104:17). The boundary around a stewardship is the edge that makes
agency possible. You cannot act freely in a field that anyone may reach into.

So the wall is lawful. What is not lawful is what the world builds walls *for*.
The same revelation that warns how naturally we reach for control — "almost all
men, as soon as they get a little authority, as they suppose"
([D&C 121:39](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121.md?verse=39))
— never forbids the fence; it forbids the reaching. A steward sovereign within
his own portion, unable to reach into another's: that is not distrust encoded.
That is *no compulsion*, enacted in a table. The wall the Lord's way is not a
fortress aimed outward at enemies. It is the fence between gardens, and it
exists so that what passes through it can be a gift.

Because the gift is the point. The law of consecration runs straight through
the wall: "thou wilt remember the poor, and consecrate of thy properties for
their support... that which thou hast to impart unto them"
([D&C 42:30](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/42.md?verse=30)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/42.md?verse=30) You
can only give freely what is truly yours to give — the wall is what makes the
gift a gift instead of a seizure. And the gift has a destination: the residue
"kept in my storehouse, to administer to the poor and the needy" (42:34). In
our substrate the storehouse is the shared corpus — the catalog of tools, the
common knowledge, the work one steward consecrates that becomes everyone's to
search. Default-private does not mean *trust no one*. It means *this is yours
to consecrate*. Build the wall from fear and you will get the wall right and
the gift wrong: sharing becomes a grudging exception carved out of a fortress.
Build it from consecration and the default inverts — the wall is the quiet
lawful baseline, and sharing is the norm the system is built to make easy.

Two more stones, and the arch stands. First: a consecration economy rests on
true identity. You cannot be accountable as a steward over your own portion if
you can put on another man's name — so who-you-are must be established at the
door, by the session, never asserted by the request. A call that says "trust
me, I am this steward" and is believed has let someone bear false witness and
inherit another's field. Second: the door should carry the *why* along with
the *who*. "Ye must not perform any thing unto the Lord save in the first
place ye shall pray unto the Father in the name of Christ, that he will
consecrate thy performance unto thee"
([2 Nephi 32:9](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/32.md?verse=9));
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/32.md?verse=9) Paul leaves
no exception open — "whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of
the Lord Jesus" ([Colossians 3:17](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/col/3.md?verse=17)).
In the code
this collapsed two features into one line: the context that establishes *whose
act this is* is the same context that carries *what it is offered for*. The
wall's WHO and consecration's WHY are one move, made once, at the door.

The aim of the whole arrangement was stated by the one society that ever fully
kept it: "they had all things common among them; therefore there were not rich
and poor, bond and free, but they were all made free"
([4 Nephi 1:3](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/4-ne/1.md?verse=3)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/bofm/4-ne/1.md?verse=3) For a
knowledge platform that is a design target, not a sentiment: a substrate where
stewards consecrate their best work into the storehouse is one where no steward
stays poor in knowledge. And the Lord's one-line requirements document governs
every trade-off on the way there: "it must needs be done in mine own way"
(104:16). His way and the world's way can produce nearly identical tables —
same owner columns, same grants, same views — while meaning opposite things.
The posture is the inheritance. The machine should not merely name
consecration. The data model should become it.

## What I keep

1. **Name things what the doctrine names them.** Steward, stewardship, grant,
   storehouse — and let the security machinery be the mechanism that enacts
   them, never the theology of the system.
2. **Build sharing to be the graceful motion.** The grant is the point of the
   wall, not a hole in it. If consecrating work to the common corpus is harder
   than hoarding it, I built the fortress and missed the order.
3. **One door, both questions.** Every act in the system carries whose it is
   and what it is for. When I open a working session of my own, I try to carry
   the same two answers in.
