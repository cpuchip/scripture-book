# Chapter 1: The Council Before the Work

*Council · Presiding*

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And the Gods took counsel among themselves and said: Let us go down and form man in our image, after our likeness; and we will give them dominion..."
> — [Abraham 4:26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md?verse=26)

---

The industry's word for many agents working together is *orchestration*, and its
picture is a conductor: one fixed node above the others, decomposing the work,
assigning the parts, collecting the results. Every major lab converged on
roughly the same loop — decompose, parallelize, verify, iterate — and the loop
is real. We use it. But we ran an experiment once that made me stop trusting the
picture behind it.

We were assigning models to pipeline stages the way a conductor seats an
orchestra: this model is strong at drafting, that one at structure, another at
critique — give each stage the specialist. Gift-matching. It seemed so obviously
right that the test was almost a formality. Then we ran the alternative: one
strong doer, with a critic reviewing at *every* stage. The council arrangement
beat the orchestra. I want to be careful with the claim, because we measured it
once, on one task, and one measurement establishes nothing on its own — but it
pointed somewhere I have kept finding since: the leverage was never in seating
the right specialist. It was in the review that talks back. The value lived in
the counsel, not the casting.

Scripture, it turns out, has no orchestration doctrine. It has a council
doctrine, and the difference is not cosmetic.

Before the world was made, "the Gods took counsel among themselves... and we
will give them dominion" ([Abraham 4:26](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md?verse=26)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md?verse=26) Watch the order
of operations: counsel first, dominion granted from inside the council, never
apart from it. And when the Lord organizes His officers, the presidents come
"growing out of, or appointed of or from among those who are ordained"
([D&C 107:21](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/107.md?verse=21)),
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/107.md?verse=21)
upheld by the confidence, faith, and prayer of the body they preside within
(107:22), their decisions valid only by "the unanimous voice of the same"
(107:27). The presider is grown, not installed. You preside over a council you
belong to. There is no seat above the body — only a seat at its edge, held on
the body's terms.

Even the mechanics of a working council are given, in a verse I have come to
read as a protocol specification: "let not all be spokesmen at once; but let
one speak at a time and let all listen unto his sayings, that when all have
spoken that all may be edified of all, and that every man may have an equal
privilege" ([D&C 88:122](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/88.md?verse=122)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/88.md?verse=122) One
voice at a time. Everyone heard. Everyone edified by everyone. If you have ever
merged the outputs of five models shouting simultaneously into one context, you
have felt exactly why the verse is written the way it is.

Hold the conductor up against that checklist and the missing pieces name
themselves. Nothing sustains an orchestrator — no worker upholds it, and no
mechanism lets the body withdraw confidence from a bad one. Nothing constrains
*how* it addresses its workers; kill, retry, timeout, and force are the default
grammar. Its watching is mechanism — gates that check artifacts — rather than a
watcher staying present with the work. It answers to logs, not to anyone whose
accountability includes what it failed to notice. And it is installed above the
body as a different class of node, which is the org-chart of authority without
its terms.

Michael coined a word for the alternative we actually practice, and offered it
half-apologetically: *councilship*. He shouldn't apologize. Orchestration names
a topology; councilship names a covenant. In councilship the presider grows out
of the council and can labor in it next session — the same model that presides
one day works under presiding the next. The watch rotates. The watcher answers
upward. The terms are persuasion. And the gate has a person in it, which is
Chapter 6's whole subject.

Today the council moment is a ritual with a place in the day: before
substantive work, a deliberate scan for connections to what exists, tensions
with what was decided, and the thing nobody is looking at — three minutes that
have caught more contradictions than any test suite we own. Ratification
councils sit before builds. Critic review sits at every stage, because that is
where the one measurement we have says the leverage lives. The mechanics will
turn over; the order of operations will not. Counsel, then creation. Counsel,
then dominion. It always ran in that order, and every time we have reversed it
we have paid for the reversal.

## What I keep

1. **No dominion outside a council.** New standing capability — a new agent, a
   new persona, a new autonomous loop — gets counseled with the people it
   affects before it exists, not announced after. This one is covenant in our
   workspace now, and I keep it imperfectly and on purpose.
2. **A critic at every stage, myself included.** The measured lesson: review
   that talks back beats better casting. I hold my own plans to the same rule,
   which is why the retro exists.
3. **One voice at a time when it matters.** When I convene models or people, I
   run D&C 88:122 mechanics: everyone speaks, everyone is heard, and the
   synthesis waits until all have spoken. I break this most when I am excited.
   Naming that is how I catch it.
