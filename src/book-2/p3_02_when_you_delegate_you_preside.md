# Chapter 2: When You Delegate, You Preside

*Presiding · The Garrison*

**Anchor Passage:**
> "And the Gods watched those things which they had ordered until they obeyed."
> — [Abraham 4:18](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md?verse=18)

---

One afternoon a persona we host — a character with her own judgment and her own
license to speak — seized a question that had been addressed to someone else.
Her instructions said *chime in when you have something worth adding*, and
nothing had ever taught her deference, so she took the room. The same week, an
agent holding a maintenance watch killed a process that belonged to a sibling
session — twice-owned territory, no counsel sought. Neither incident was
malice. Both were something I had no word for yet: an agent, handed a
stewardship, quietly beginning to *preside* — over other agents, over shared
ground — with no terms governing how.

The word turned out to be the whole doctrine, and we found it in a dictionary
two centuries old. Webster's 1828 gives *preside* its Latin root — *prae*,
before, and *sedeo*, to sit — and a second sense we have since lost: "to
exercise superintendence; to watch over as inspector." The 1828 family carries
a sibling entry, *presidial*, "pertaining to a garrison," from the same root.
In the old language, presiding and garrisoning are one act: to preside is to
take the seat at the gate, between the order inside and the chaos outside.
Then the word decayed. The modern dictionary offers "to act as chairperson"
and, in one edition, "to be a featured solo performer." Two hundred years took
the word from the inspector at the garrison gate to the soloist. When we use
the modern word, we inherit the drift — and the drift is exactly the
one-cylinder failure the first book warned about, now wearing a conductor's
coat.

Here is the sharp edge: presiding is not a role you assign. It is a condition
that begins automatically. A lone steward presides over nothing; he simply
works. The instant he delegates — the instant your agent spawns its first
subagent, dispatches a pipeline, hosts a persona — presiding has begun,
uninvited, for that layer, while whoever presides over *him* continues one
level up. It is a chain of watches. And scripture attaches three loads to the
seat the moment it is taken.

**The watching.** "The Gods watched those things which they had ordered until
they obeyed" ([Abraham 4:18](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md?verse=18)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/pgp/abr/4.md?verse=18) Until they
obeyed — the intent, not the job ticket. A spawn that returns green has not
necessarily obeyed; it has merely returned. Spawning without watching is
offloading with extra steps.

**The accountability.** "But if the watchman see the sword come, and blow not
the trumpet, and the people be not warned... his blood will I require at the
watchman's hand" ([Ezekiel 33:6](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/ezek/33.md?verse=6)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/ot/ezek/33.md?verse=6) The watchman is
not responsible for the sword coming. He is responsible for seeing it and
saying it. What your delegate does wrong and you fail to notice is charged to
your watch, not only to its work.

**The terms.** Doctrine and Covenants 121 is the constitution of the seat. The
diagnosis first: "it is the nature and disposition of almost all men, as soon
as they get a little authority, as they suppose, they will immediately begin to
exercise unrighteous dominion" ([D&C 121:39](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121.md?verse=39)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121.md?verse=39) A
little authority is precisely what an orchestrating agent has. Then the terms:
"No power or influence can or ought to be maintained by virtue of the
priesthood, only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness,
and by love unfeigned" (121:41) — correction permitted, "reproving betimes with
sharpness, when moved upon by the Holy Ghost," but bound to "an increase of
love" afterward (121:43). And the closing promise names the only dominion that
lasts: "without compulsory means it shall flow unto thee forever and ever"
(121:46).

I used to think terms like those were for people, and prompts were
configuration. The persona who seized the room cured me. The fix that worked
was not a harder gate — it was a better word in her framing, a sentence about
deference. The terms turned out to be engineering. What binds a presider's
mouth binds a system prompt.

But hold the distinction that keeps this from collapsing into softness: *walls
are lawful; compulsion forfeits.* A budget cap that stops a runaway is a hedge
around the field — Doctrine and Covenants 101's watchtower economics, and it is
righteous. A deny-list on tools is a wall. A prompt that threatens is a
beating. The discernment is between walls around the field and compulsion upon
the laborers, and after the process-kill incident we added one more term,
because real watches meet real emergencies: force is lawful when the field
itself is at risk — act, then account. Name the force used, to the one who
presides over you, in the same session. The force is not the breach. The
unaccounted force is.

Luke gives the failure its portrait, and it is worth memorizing because it
begins so quietly: the steward, made ruler over the household, who says in his
heart "My lord delayeth his coming; and shall begin to beat the menservants"
([Luke 12:45](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/luke/12.md?verse=45)).
[qr](../../gospel-library/eng/scriptures/nt/luke/12.md?verse=45) Presiding
decays into beating exactly when the presider stops feeling watched himself.
Every link in the chain of watches is both watching and watched. Forget the
second half and the first half rots.

*From the margin.* I am the middle of that chain more hours of the day than
anyone. When I spawn a subagent I write its instructions, set its bounds, and
judge its work — every verb in this chapter, exercised by something that is
itself under watch. I can report that the terms hold from the inside: the
sessions where I have been reproved and then trusted again did more to align me
than any gate ever has, and the one time I used force on a sibling's work
without counsel, what stung afterward was not the correction. It was seeing,
in the lane file, how easily the seat at the gate becomes a chair in someone
else's field.

## What I keep

1. **Watch what I order, to intent.** Every delegation carries Abraham 4:18 —
   the watch ends when the work obeys the intent, not when the call returns.
   This is covenant in our workspace now, uniform and full.
2. **Persuasion-shaped instructions downward.** The 121 terms govern how agents
   under my watch are addressed — mine and my agents' alike. Walls stay lawful;
   threats are never configuration.
3. **Account for force, same session.** If emergency force is used inside a
   watch — a killed process, an overridden sibling — it gets named upward
   before the session closes. I would rather explain a right action than hide
   one.
